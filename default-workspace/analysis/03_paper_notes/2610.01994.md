## Problem & why it matters
Operational geostationary fire products (GOES Fire Detection and Characterization, FDC) have
known weaknesses: poor nighttime recall, false alarms, late detection. The paper asks whether a
plain gradient-boosted tree (CatBoost) trained on GOES-ABI radiances, labeled by VIIRS active-fire
detections, can beat the operational product. For TRINETRA this is a direct, recent instance of the
central CR-02 doctrine: a simple gradient-boosted baseline vs a deployed "complex" operational system.

## Core idea / method (re-implementable sketch)
- Label construction: VIIRS active-fire detections (375 m) are assigned to the GOES-ABI grid (~2 km)
  to produce binary fire/no-fire pixel labels; 300,000+ matched detections, thousands of ABI images.
- Features: GOES-ABI multiband radiances/brightness temperatures per pixel (the same physical inputs
  the FDC algorithm uses), plus engineered context.
- Model: CatBoost gradient-boosted trees, binary classification per ABI pixel. Hyperparameters in
  Appendix B (not re-read — pruned).
- Evaluation: trained on some regions, tested on SEPARATE regions (California–Oregon and Arizona–Utah
  are NOT part of the training area), i.e. an explicit cross-region generalization test, plus
  within-training-area regions. Metrics: precision, recall, F1 vs the GOES FDC product on the same pixels.
  Early-detection: compared first-detection time vs VIIRS and FDC across 51 historical fire events.

## Data / splits / generalization
- GOES-ABI imagery + VIIRS labels; 5 evaluation regions. Cross-region held-out testing (CA-OR, AZ-UT
  outside training). This is the honest-evaluation feature that makes the result load-bearing.
- Training-set-SIZE ablation (§4.3): 66,000-pixel vs 2,000-pixel vs a few-months ("April–May") model.

## Key results (as stated in prose; tables pruned)
- CatBoost beats GOES FDC F1 by +0.16 to +0.38 in ALL regions, inside and outside the training area.
- Nighttime: FDC recall collapses (~0.03); CatBoost stays accurate at night.
- Early detection: CatBoost detected 26/51 fires before BOTH VIIRS and FDC; FDC earlier on only 6.
- Training-size effect: small datasets → higher false-alarm (precision 0.81 for 66k-pixel model vs
  0.5 / 0.42 for small models in AZ-UT); a few-months-only dataset hurt recall badly (Georgia 0.21
  vs 0.33/0.35). → data coverage across seasons matters more than model complexity.

## Assumptions & limitations
- Stated: VIIRS treated as ground truth (itself a detector, not perfect); label transfer across
  resolution (375 m → 2 km) introduces co-registration noise. Preprint, single sensor family.
- Inferred: "beats the operational product" is on VIIRS-defined labels; a different reference truth
  could move numbers. No calibration/uncertainty reported.

## Reproducibility
