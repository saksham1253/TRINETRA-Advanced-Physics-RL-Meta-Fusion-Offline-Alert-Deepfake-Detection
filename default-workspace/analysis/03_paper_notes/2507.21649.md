## Problem & why it matters
Companion survey to 2508.14203 but organized around the DNN→MLLM TRANSITION and a UNIFIED task
formalism (anomaly understanding vs anomaly grounding) across supervision levels + input modalities.
Gives TRINETRA a precise vocabulary for what the WS4 video module should output and a modality menu
(RGB / optical flow / skeleton / hybrid) with trade-offs.

## Core content (reusable parts)
- Formalism: train on videos X_train (H×W×C frames), optimize Φ(θ) against a discrepancy P between
  predictions and labels (eq.1–2); at inference score X_test against learned normality.
- Task split: Video Anomaly UNDERSTANDING (semantic/explanatory — MLLM territory, heavy) vs
  Video Anomaly GROUNDING (localize anomalous frames/regions — detector territory, light).
- Supervision taxonomy: semi-supervised (self-supervised + one-class), weakly-supervised,
  fully/unsupervised.
- Input-modality menu for semi-supervised VAD: RGB (appearance), OPTICAL FLOW (motion — robust to
  appearance change), SKELETON (pose, privacy-preserving + cheap), HYBRID. Optical flow + skeleton
  are explicitly cheaper/more-robust than RGB for motion anomalies.
- Datasets/metrics: UCF-Crime, XD-Violence, ShanghaiTech, etc.; frame-AUC the dominant metric.

## Key takeaways (survey-level)
- MLLM methods add explanation ("understanding") at large compute cost; grounding/detection remains
  the deployable core. Confirms WS4 doctrine: edge node needs a LIGHT grounding detector, not an MLLM.
- Optical-flow / skeleton inputs are a cheap robustness + privacy lever for crowd/river scenes.
