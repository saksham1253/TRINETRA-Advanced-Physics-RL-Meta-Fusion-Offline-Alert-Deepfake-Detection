## What it is
Classic probabilistic shielding needs the MDP's **transition probabilities a priori** — unavailable
in real RL. This paper assumes the **transition GRAPH is known but probabilities are UNKNOWN**, and
**learns them online** as the agent explores; from the running estimate it (re)computes a shield.
The shield is **conservative early, tightens as the estimate sharpens** — "the shield improves in
tandem with the RL agent." Uses interval-MDP machinery. Open challenges they name: WHEN to recompute
the shield, and how to trade exploration vs safety.

## Why it matters to TRINETRA (WS5 safety shield, CR-03)
- Our current safety shield (Alshiekh 1708.08611 style) uses **hard, hand-specified physics
  overrides** (FS<1→Orange, tilt>5°/day→Red, 24h rain>150mm→Orange, GLOF sig→Red). This paper is the
  complementary case: when the environment's probabilities are **learned, not known**, the shield
  must be **conservative under model uncertainty and adapt** — directly relevant because our hazard
  transition probabilities ARE unknown and estimated from scarce data.
- **Design doctrine for WS5:** keep BOTH layers — (a) the fixed physics override (never relaxes;
  safety-of-last-resort, Rule 19), and (b) an *adaptive* probabilistic shield that starts
  conservative when data is thin and only loosens as calibration improves. Early in deployment
  (little local data) the alarm policy should be **more trigger-happy**, tightening as evidence
  accrues — the exact opposite of over-trusting a freshly trained model.
- "Transition graph known, probabilities unknown" matches our setting well: we know the hazard STATE
  structure (quiescent→precursor→failure) from physics; we don't know the rates.
- Reinforces that the shield recomputation schedule + exploration-safety tradeoff are explicit
  design parameters, not afterthoughts.
