## Content (own words)
- Problem: public agencies must spend limited resilience funds BEFORE the location/severity of
  future floods is known — a strategic capital-allocation problem under uncertainty, with
  consequences unevenly distributed across communities.
- Method: BI-OBJECTIVE TWO-STAGE STOCHASTIC optimization. First stage = permanent facility
  hardening decisions (here-and-now, before the hazard). Second stage = scenario-dependent
  evacuation decisions (recourse, after the hazard is realized). Objective 1 = minimize EXPECTED
  (evacuation + physical-damage + business-interruption) cost. Objective 2 = minimize a service-
  disruption impact index combining duration × scale of service loss weighted by a PLACE-BASED
  SOCIAL-VULNERABILITY weight. Solved exactly via multi-cut Benders decomposition + a scalable
  Lagrangian-dual method, inside an adaptive ε-constrained Pareto-frontier procedure.
- Verified findings (abstract/intro): case study of 3,752 Texas hospitals/nursing homes under
  climate-informed tropical-cyclone flood scenarios. KEY RESULT: minimizing ECONOMIC LOSS ALONE
  systematically under-protects facilities in socially VULNERABLE areas; moderate movement along
  the Pareto frontier substantially reduces disruption impact at limited extra expected cost.

## What it CHANGED in our thinking (required for quota)
- Fills gap G3 with a rigorous template for the "value of a warning / what to do with it" layer.
  TRINETRA produces people-at-risk → someone must DECIDE evacuate/shelter under uncertainty. This
  paper shows the right framing: a TWO-STAGE structure where our alarm/forecast feeds the
  SECOND-STAGE recourse (evacuation), while first-stage = pre-positioned mitigation. Our RL alarm
  reward (disruption = −0.01/person-hour) is a crude scalarization of exactly their cost terms;
  this paper argues for MULTI-OBJECTIVE treatment and a Pareto frontier rather than one fixed
  weight. ACTION: in P15 reward design, expose the disruption-vs-safety tradeoff as a frontier /
  tunable, not a single hard-coded coefficient.
- STRONGEST equity lesson for Rule 19: optimizing pure expected economic cost SYSTEMATICALLY
  disadvantages socially vulnerable communities. For a system meant to protect Himalayan
  communities (many poor, remote), a naive cost-minimizing alarm policy could under-serve exactly
  the people most at risk. ACTION: add an explicit SOCIAL-VULNERABILITY weight to the people-at-
  risk / disruption accounting and flag it in every design doc as an equity requirement for
  expert review — a concrete, defensible instantiation of Rule 19 beyond generic "human oversight".
- Methodologically: two-stage stochastic programming + Benders is a principled ALTERNATIVE/
  complement to RL for the decision layer — worth noting against CR-03 (sometimes classical
  stochastic optimization, not RL, is the right tool for the recourse decision).
