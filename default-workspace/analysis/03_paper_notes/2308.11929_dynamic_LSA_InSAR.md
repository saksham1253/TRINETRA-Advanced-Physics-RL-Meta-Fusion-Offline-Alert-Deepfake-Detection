## Content (own words)
- Problem: landslide susceptibility assessment (LSA) must cope with a landslide-inducing
  environment (LIE) that OSCILLATES over time (esp. rainfall), but most data-driven LSA
  fits one static black-box model → poor temporal generalization + small-sample years + no
  explanation.
- Core idea (re-implementable): (1) DYNAMIC LSA = separate annual predictive models (here
  1992–2019); (2) META-LEARN representations with fast adaptation (few samples + few gradient
  updates) to handle small-sample years; (3) SHAP + feature permutation for per-model
  interpretation; (4) MT-InSAR (multi-temporal InSAR, coherence-weighted phase-linking,
  distributed-scatterer points via temporal-coherence threshold) to ENHANCE and VALIDATE the
  susceptibility map — because optical-interpreted inventories are post-failure-biased and
  miss slow pre-failure slope movement.
- Findings seen: primary triggers = terrain slope + extreme rainfall; temporal variation in
  causes driven by climate-change-linked extreme rainfall + the HK LPMitP mitigation program.
  Proposed method beats baselines even with fast adaptation.
