## Content (own words)
- Problem: standard conformal prediction (CP) assumes data EXCHANGEABILITY, which TIME SERIES
  violate (temporal dependence, non-stationarity). High-stakes sequential settings (energy,
  supply chain) need distribution-free prediction INTERVALS with provable coverage anyway.
- Method: **EnbPI** (Ensemble batch Prediction Intervals) — wraps around any ENSEMBLE of
  point predictors (bootstrap/bagged); builds intervals from the empirical CDF of leave-one-out
  (out-of-bag) residuals, updated sequentially. Key properties: does NOT require data-splitting
  (uses OOB residuals, so no held-out calibration set wasted), does NOT require retraining as new
  points arrive (scalable to streaming), and does NOT require exchangeability.
- Theory (visible): explicit bounds on CONDITIONAL and MARGINAL coverage gaps that asymptotically
  converge to zero under stated assumptions (error-process regularity + estimation quality);
  similar bounds on set-difference vs oracle intervals. Coverage-gap controlled via the gap
  between estimated residual CDF F̂ and true F.
- Evidence: extensive simulation + real-data analyses showing EnbPI matches/beats prior interval
  methods, especially under non-stationarity.

## What it CHANGED in our thinking (required for quota)
- This is the MISSING METHOD for CR-04 and gap G1: TRINETRA's InSAR deformation series, rain,
  tilt, and soil-moisture are all AUTOCORRELATED, non-exchangeable time series — plain
  split-conformal (implied by 2107.07511) is NOT valid on them. EnbPI gives a principled,
  distribution-free way to put CALIBRATED prediction intervals on our forecast-residual anomaly
  scores and on people-at-risk inputs, WITHOUT the exchangeability assumption we were implicitly
  (and wrongly) relying on. ACTION: adopt EnbPI (or an EnbPI-style OOB-residual scheme) as the
  conformal layer baseline for the GMF head's per-zone Mondrian conformal; downgrades the report's
  blanket "conformal guarantee" (Bug #13) to the HONEST statement: coverage holds approximately,
  with an explicit gap that shrinks only under stated regularity — NOT a hard guarantee.
- Two practical wins for our constraints: (1) no data-splitting → we keep scarce labelled events
  for evaluation instead of calibration; (2) no retraining per step → cheap on Kaggle and
  deployable on a gateway. Both matter given ~25-30 GPU-h/week.
- Caveat it forces us to state: coverage degrades when the residual process is highly
  non-stationary / under abrupt distribution shift — exactly our drift concern (FM-07/FM-14). So
  EnbPI must be paired with drift monitoring + the monotone safety shield; conformal alone is not
  a safety guarantee (Rule 19). Opens a sub-question: adaptive-CP / online-CP for shift (the same
  search returned adaptive methods — carry to P2 A7/A14).
