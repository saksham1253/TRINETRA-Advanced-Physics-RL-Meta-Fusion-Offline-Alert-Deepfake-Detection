## What it is
An end-to-end **landslide early-warning (LEWS)** system that explicitly confronts the fact that LEWS
inputs are driven by **UNCERTAIN rainfall FORECASTS**, not observed rainfall — the operational reality
TRINETRA faces. Two coupled models:
1. **Rainfall Forecasting Model (§III-A):** produces the rainfall input (with inherent forecast error).
2. **Landslide Prediction Model (§III-B):** consumes rainfall + ground-motion/displacement to predict
   landslide risk. Its core novelty is **Rainfall-Motion-Aware Contrastive Learning (RMCL)** — a
   contrastive objective that learns representations ROBUST to displacement/forecast perturbations, so
   the predictor doesn't flip when the rainfall forecast is noisy.
- Explicitly addresses **class imbalance** (landslides are rare positives) as a first-class problem
  (§II-B) in both representation and loss design.
- Evaluation (§IV): real data; compared against SOTA LEWS/displacement-prediction baselines; RMCL
  ablation isolates the contrastive component's contribution.
- Related work cites Lombardo spatio-temporal susceptibility→risk forecast and attention-based
  teacher-student displacement prediction with weather forecast — situating it in the modern LEWS line.

## Why it matters to TRINETRA (directly addresses two of our hardest, most under-covered problems)
- **Forecast-uncertainty propagation is a core TRINETRA gap this paper tackles head-on.** Our WHEN layer
  depends on GPM IMERG / forecast precipitation, which is UNCERTAIN; naively feeding a point rainfall
  forecast into the alarm policy is exactly the brittleness RMCL guards against. The design lesson:
  learn representations that are INVARIANT to plausible rainfall-forecast perturbations, so the alarm
  does not oscillate with forecast noise. This complements our calibrated-uncertainty stack (CP) with a
  REPRESENTATION-level robustness mechanism — a distinct, additive idea.
- **Displacement + rainfall FUSION for landslides** is TRINETRA's exact InSAR(displacement) + IMERG
  (rainfall) coupling. RMCL is a concrete contrastive recipe to fuse these two so the motion signal and
  the (uncertain) rainfall signal reinforce rather than fight — a candidate GMF sub-objective for the
  landslide head, pairing with Anantrasirichai displacement front-end (2005.03221) + Caine I–D
  thresholds.
- **Class imbalance as first-class** aligns with our rare-event doctrine (Bayes p*=0.0476, cost-loss,
  rare-class-signal protection thread K). Confirms that any landslide learner must build imbalance
  handling into representation + loss, not just resample.
- **Two-stage (forecast model → prediction model)** mirrors the MW-Nowcast decomposition and our
  structure-then-residual doctrine: the rainfall forecaster supplies deterministic structure; the
  landslide model must be robust to its residual error. Reinforces Thread I ("physics/forecast as
  structure, learner robust to residual").
