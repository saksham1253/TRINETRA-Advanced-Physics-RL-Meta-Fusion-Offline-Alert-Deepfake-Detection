## Content (own words)
- Problem: smart-city sensor networks must be reliable, scalable, real-time, low-cost, easy to
  install/maintain — all heavily determined by two design choices: CONNECTIVITY and POWER.
  Few long real-world studies exist.
- Study: a stationary 118-node LTE-connected, SOLAR-powered sensor network over ONE YEAR in
  Chicago. Analyzes networking + powering performance.
- Evidence (verified from abstract): 11 sites had inadequate received signal strength (RSS)
  to support nodes; OVER 33,000 node-hours of DATA LOSS due to solar-energy availability
  Oct–March (low-sun season). Neighborhoods worst hit by connectivity + charging were
  socioeconomically disadvantaged areas — an EQUITY finding. Analyzes land use, adaptive
  energy-harvesting management, and open-data shortcomings.

## Relevance to us
- Component: WS7 (comms + power) + system reliability. STRONG, sobering transfer: TRINETRA's
  nodes face a HARSHER version of both failures — (1) CONNECTIVITY: Himalayan valleys have
  patchy/no cellular → this validates our LoRa/LPWAN + store-and-forward DTN choice over LTE,
  and warns that even LoRa will have dead sites needing a link budget + relay planning; (2)
  POWER: 33k hours of winter solar data-loss in CHICAGO implies our monsoon-cloud + winter
  Himalaya nodes will have WORSE solar availability → oversize panels/batteries, add adaptive
  duty-cycling, and treat data gaps as expected (the pipeline must handle missing-node inputs —
