## Content (own words)
- Problem: in high-stakes settings you must estimate a new policy's value from a LOGGED dataset
  (off-policy evaluation, OPE) because you can't just deploy to see what happens. Prior work
  improved the OPE ESTIMATOR; this paper argues the DATASET is the binding constraint — sometimes
  no estimator can evaluate your policy on your data.
- Method: DataCOPE, a data-centric framework that (1) FORECASTS how well OPE algorithms will do
  WITHOUT environment access (usable before deployment, when you can't check OPE); (2) IDENTIFIES
  sub-groups where OPE will be INACCURATE; (3) lets you compare datasets / data-collection
  strategies for their OPE-suitability. Validated on logged contextual-bandit healthcare data
  (evaluating both ML policies and human-expert clinical guidelines) and scaled to LLM reward
  modeling.

## What it CHANGED in our thinking (required for quota)
- This is the missing META-LAYER for CR-03 and our whole offline-RL alarm-policy plan. We planned
  to learn an alarm policy offline (CQL/IQL/TD3+BC or one-step) and evaluate it with OPE before
  trusting it. DataCOPE says: FIRST check whether our logged alarm/outcome dataset can even
  SUPPORT reliable OPE, and WHERE (which zones/regimes) it cannot. Given our data is sparse,
  rare-event, and spatially heterogeneous, OPE will almost certainly be unreliable in exactly the
  high-consequence tail — and DataCOPE gives a principled way to SHOW that rather than assume it.
  ACTION: add a mandatory "OPE-feasibility / coverage" gate before any offline-RL policy claim —
  i.e., do not report a learned policy's value where the data cannot support the estimate; fall
  back to the shielded fixed-threshold/LHASA baseline there. Strengthens CR-03's "prefer simple"
  resolution with a concrete diagnostic.
- Reinforces the honest-coverage theme shared with EnbPI (2010.09107, conformal) and the safety
  case (2209.11632): report WHERE guarantees/estimates hold and WHERE they don't, per sub-group
  (per-zone) — the per-zone Mondrian idea in GMF now has company at the policy-evaluation level.
- The causal/observational caveat (Pearl: limits of what observational data can tell you) is a
  direct warning against over-trusting any value we compute for an un-deployed alarm policy —
  consistent with Rule 16 (no claims about OUR system performance until built & validated).
