## Content (own words)
- Problem: off-policy value-based RL fails offline because bootstrapping from
  out-of-distribution (OOD) actions causes over-optimistic Q-values under the dataset-vs-
  policy distributional shift.
- Core idea (re-implementable): learn a CONSERVATIVE Q-function that lower-bounds the true
  value. Augment the standard Bellman error with a regularizer that pushes DOWN Q-values on
  a (policy/uniform) action distribution and pulls UP Q-values on dataset actions. Eq.1 gives
  a pointwise lower bound on Q^π; Eq.2 (expected-value variant) gives a tighter bound on the
  policy's expected value. Drop-in on top of DQN / SAC-style actor-critic. Single extra
  coefficient alpha.
- Theory seen: with suitable alpha, both bounds hold under sampling error + function
  approximation; bound tightens as dataset coverage |D(s,a)| grows.
- Data/baselines/metrics: abstract claims 2–5x higher final return vs prior offline methods
  on discrete + continuous control, strongest on multimodal data. (Exact D4RL numbers were
  in the pruned middle — NOT verified here.)

## Relevance to us
- Component: WS5 RL alarm policy. CQL is one of the 4 offline-RL candidates in the TRINETRA
  design (CQL/IQL/TD3+BC). Takeaway: conservatism/lower-bounding is the mechanism that keeps
  an offline alarm policy from hallucinating value for never-observed alarm actions in rare
  disaster states — directly relevant to safety.
- Conflict: feeds CR-03 (multi-stage vs one-step offline RL). CQL is iterative/regularized;
  2106.08909 argues one-step can match it at lower variance. Resolve by benchmarking.
- Transfer hypothesis: D4RL (robotics/locomotion) ≠ disaster alarm MDP; whether CQL's gains
