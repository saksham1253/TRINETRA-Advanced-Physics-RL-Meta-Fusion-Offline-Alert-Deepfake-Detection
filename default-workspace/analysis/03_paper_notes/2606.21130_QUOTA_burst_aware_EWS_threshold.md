## Content (own words)
- Problem: bursty AI workloads push data centers into capacity-stress states too fast for
  REACTIVE static-threshold mechanisms. They want PROACTIVE warning before SLO violation.
- Method: frame as a HIGH-RECALL forecasting task over multivariate telemetry windows; a
  LIGHTWEIGHT TREE-BASED (XGBoost) model over workload-intensity + temporal-variation + system-
  pressure features; plus a surge-injection methodology to simulate bursts for evaluation.
  Deployment-oriented THRESHOLD selection chosen to trade recall vs false-alarm cost explicitly.
- Verified numbers (abstract + §I): surge injection raises capacity-stress rate by 37.9% (§I body,
  not the abstract). XGBoost model: ROC AUC 0.697, Average Precision 0.670 (abstract), "significantly outperforming baselines." Under
  the deployment threshold: Recall 0.914 (catches most stress periods) "with acceptable
  false-alarm cost." Integrated into control loops for throttling/scaling.

## What it CHANGED in our thinking (required for quota)
- A clean, recent template for exactly TRINETRA's operating-point problem: it does NOT pick a
  threshold to maximize accuracy/F1; it picks the threshold on the recall–false-alarm tradeoff
  tuned to the OPERATIONAL cost — the same philosophy as our Bayes p*=C_FA/(C_FA+C_miss) and the
  high-recall, miss-averse stance. Confirms our instinct that for life-safety we accept more false
  alarms for higher recall — but it also quantifies the honest cost: even a decent model here caps
  at AUC 0.697 / AP 0.670. That is a sober reference: ML EWS on hard imbalanced telemetry gives
  MODEST discrimination, and the system's value comes from THRESHOLD POLICY + control-loop
  integration, not from a magic AUC. Reinforces CR-02 and the "simple tree + good operating point"
  doctrine (2608.12422's 3-rule tree, the LHASA lineage).
- The reactive-threshold critique maps onto our safety shield design: static thresholds alone are
  too slow/brittle → learned forecasting PLUS a threshold policy PLUS a hard override is the right
  layering. Supports keeping the shield (1708.08611) as hard floor while the learned layer buys
  lead time, rather than replacing thresholds entirely.
- The surge-INJECTION evaluation methodology is a transferable idea: we cannot wait for real
  GLOFs/landslides to test, so a (clearly-labelled, simulation-only, research-note) stress-injection
  protocol for evaluation is worth designing — but NOTE §0.1A: actual simulation is build-work,
  deferred; here it is only a methodological pointer for a future phase.
