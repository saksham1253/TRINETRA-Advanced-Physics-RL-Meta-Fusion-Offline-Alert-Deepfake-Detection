## What it is
**GNN-rP** = a hybrid of **graph neural networks** + **r-Pareto processes** (a peaks-over-threshold
extreme-value-theory construct) for modeling and ZONING extreme-precipitation risk. The explicit
motivation is our compound-hazard chain: "extreme precipitation ... can trigger compound flooding,
LANDSLIDES, and infrastructure failures across wide areas."
- **Spatial graph:** nodes = grid cells; edges from precipitation-derived spatial proximity/dependence.
- **Node features** x_i = [μ, σ, max, threshold-exceedance-frequency] of daily precipitation per year —
  i.e. summary stats INCLUDING a POT exceedance frequency.
- **Graph-convolutional embedding** learns nonlinear, NONSTATIONARY spatial dependence (vs traditional
  stationary spatial-extremes models).
- **Embedding-based tail functional + r-Pareto features:** a data-driven tail functional models JOINT
  exceedances (co-occurring extremes across locations) in a low-dimensional embedding space — this is the
  EVT core: it models the TAIL (rare, high-impact events), not the bulk.
- **Risk zoning:** delineates coherent high-risk zones, quantifies temporal persistence, detects emerging
  hotspots under climate change.
- **Data:** NASA **IMERG** (2000–2021) observations + **CMIP6 SSP5-8.5** projections.

## Why it matters to TRINETRA (fills the EVT/tail-risk gap)
1. **First extreme-value-theory paper in the corpus — fills a real hole.** Our hazards are by definition
   TAIL events (rare, extreme). Everything we've read so far models the bulk (classification/regression/
   anomaly); none explicitly modeled the TAIL with EVT. GNN-rP shows how to combine a learned
   spatial embedding with a principled POT/r-Pareto tail model. → Add an **EVT/tail-risk thread** to the
   design: our precipitation WHEN layer and our people-at-risk estimate should carry an explicit tail
   model, not just a mean forecast. This is a genuine new design axis surfaced by SOTA reading.
2. **Uses IMERG — our exact precipitation input.** Directly reusable feature idea: per-cell [μ, σ, max,
   threshold-exceedance-frequency] over a window as node features on a spatial graph. Cheap, offline-
   computable from IMERG, and interpretable. Candidate feature set for the WHEN layer / zone definition.
3. **Spatial graph with nonstationary dependence** is directly relevant to our per-zone (Mondrian
   conformal) design: a GNN over catchment/zone nodes could model cross-zone dependence of extreme rain
   (upstream→downstream), which a per-zone-independent model misses. HYPOTHESIS to test.
4. **Compound-hazard framing (rain → flood + landslide together)** matches our multi-hazard fusion thesis;
   the "joint exceedance" idea (multiple locations/hazards exceeding simultaneously) is exactly the
   correlated-failure case our GMF + safety-shield must handle (compound events, not independent).
5. **Two-baseline comparison + honest "comparable (not better) clustering stability"** = the fair-reporting
   discipline we require (Thread H); they do NOT overclaim.
6. **Climate-projection (SSP5-8.5) persistence** supports our deployment framing: high-risk zones are
   non-stationary and shifting under climate change → the system must be re-calibratable over time, not
   frozen (connects to drift/robustness WS6).

## Caveats / honesty
- **GLOBAL/coarse resolution + climate-zoning purpose**, NOT operational short-term warning. This is a
  strategic risk-ZONING tool (where/persistently), not a WHEN-trigger nowcaster. Use it for zone
  definition / prior risk, not for the real-time alarm. Do not conflate the two.
- stat.AP paper; r-Pareto / tail-functional machinery adds statistical complexity and estimation
  variance in data-sparse mountain cells — EVT tail estimates are notoriously unstable with few
  exceedances. In the Himalaya with sparse records, tail parameters would be high-variance; treat EVT
  outputs as HYPOTHESIS with wide uncertainty, never a hard threshold.
- Two-baseline improvement is on "pointwise detection of high-risk grid cells" (a zoning metric), not on
  event-level warning skill; cite accordingly (Rule 16).
- CMIP6 SSP5-8.5 projections are scenario-dependent; persistence findings are conditional on that scenario.
