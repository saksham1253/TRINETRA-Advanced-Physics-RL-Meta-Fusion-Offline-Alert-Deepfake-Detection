## Content (own words)
- Problem: critical cyber-physical systems (water networks, power grids) generate IMBALANCED
  MULTI-CLASS data — rare faults/attacks vs abundant normal. Class imbalance biases models toward
  majority classes and makes high-impact rare events hard to detect. Weighted-voting ENSEMBLES
  are standard, but existing weighting schemes don't optimally balance per-class classifier
  contributions, and edge deployment needs SMALL ensembles (resource-constrained devices).
- Method: a MIXED-INTEGER PROGRAMMING (MIP) scheme that optimizes weights of classifier-CLASS
  PAIRS (granular, per-class, not just per-classifier), with elastic-net regularization for
  robustness/generalization, AND simultaneously SELECTS a predefined number of classifiers from a
  larger pool (so you can cap ensemble size for the edge).
- Verified findings (abstract + visible tables): vs six established weighting schemes across
  representative datasets and ensemble sizes K=2..8, MIP improves balanced accuracy by 0.99-7.31%
  (avg 4.53%) and macro precision/recall/F1 by ~4.6% each, while staying computationally
  efficient. Datasets include CIC-IDS2017 (imbalance ratio ρ=2.13) and small custom CPS attack
  sets (per-class counts shown in Table 1).
