## Why it's here (the transferable methodology, NOT the finance)
A hard, quantitative demonstration of how **deep-RL conclusions can be artefacts of training-seed
luck and specification choices** — exactly the trap our WS5 alarm-policy evaluation must avoid.

## Key findings (as-published)
- DRL order-execution policies **vary substantially across training seeds** → apparent architectural
  gains may be favourable training realisations, not reproducible properties.
- **No learned config beat simple baselines**: all had higher mean implementation shortfall than
  TWAP (0.39 bps) and immediate liquidation (0.21 bps). *(Another CR-02 datapoint: simple baselines
  win.)*
- Failure mode: 11/100 vanilla-DDQL runs collapse to "wait until forced liquidation"; 0 in MoE K≥4.
- BUT decomposition shows the MoE "fix" is illusory: **annealed exploration ALONE eliminates
  collapse** (12/100→0/100, McNemar p=4.9e−4) — MoE "masks a training-specification failure rather
  than conferring an intrinsic benefit."
- **THE headline for us:** "The apparent attribution of the failure mode **reverses between 30 and
  100 seeds**, illustrating the importance of repeated-seed evaluation." Within-policy tail risk
  worsens monotonically with K; across-seed dispersion non-monotone and not robust to family-wise
  adjustment.

## Why it matters to TRINETRA (WS5 eval protocol + RB risk)
- **Mandates a rigorous RL eval protocol** for our alarm policy, to be written into the WS5 /
  eval-harness spec:
  1. **Many seeds** (≥100, not 3–30) — conclusions can literally reverse between 30 and 100 seeds.
  2. Report **tail risk + across-seed dispersion**, not just mean reward (echoes PIER 2603.17319).
  3. **Always compare against trivial baselines** (fixed threshold + persistence) — our hardened
     TWO-BASELINE rule; here even TWAP/immediate beat all RL.
  4. Beware "architectural gains" that are really **masked training-spec failures** — ablate the
     specification (exploration schedule, reward alignment) before crediting an architecture.
- → New build-risk RB-13 (RL eval validity) and a direct input to the eval-harness.
