## What it is
A DL workflow for JOINT **flood + landslide** multi-hazard susceptibility mapping (FL-MHSM) at REGIONAL
scale, evaluated in **Kerala (India)** and **Nepal** — directly our hazard pair and our geography.
- **Two-level spatial partitioning:** contextual zones (ESZ-based in Kerala, NNH/glacier-based in Nepal)
  + overlapping lattice grid computational units → preserves spatial heterogeneity + data-parallel
  large-area prediction. **Zone-wise feature selection** (different conditioning factors per zone).
- **Three fusion designs compared:** probabilistic **Early Fusion (EF)**, tree-based **Late Fusion (LF)
  baseline**, and a **soft-gating Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)** as the FINAL model.
- **Results (as published):**
  - Kerala: EF competitive w/ LF (flood recall 0.816→0.840, Brier 0.092→0.086); **MoE best for flood**
    (AUC-ROC 0.905, recall 0.930, F1 0.722).
  - Nepal: EF improves flood recall 0.820→0.858, Brier 0.057→0.049 vs LF; **MoE best for landslide**
    (AUC-ROC 0.914, recall 0.901, F1 0.559).
  - **GeoDetector** interpretation: dominant factors VARY across zones (Kerala topo/land-cover/drainage
    mix; Nepal more consistent topographic + glacier controls).

## Why it matters to TRINETRA (GMF gating validated on OUR hazards/region; spatial-CV; calibration-first; honest F1)
- **Strongest external precedent yet for the GMF design on our exact problem.** FL-MHSM's **soft-gating
  MoE** is a direct cousin of our Gated Meta-Fusion head: a learned gate routes between per-expert
  predictions, and it WINS over both early- and late-fusion baselines for the headline hazard in each
  region. This is concrete, same-domain evidence that **gated fusion > fixed fusion** for
  flood/landslide — while still honoring CR-02 because they explicitly BENCHMARK against EF and LF
  baselines (exactly the "earn it over baselines" discipline).
- **Spatial partitioning + zone-wise features = our per-zone Mondrian / zonal-normal doctrine.** They
  find dominant conditioning factors differ by zone (Kerala) — empirical support for our decision to
  make the fusion/conformal/normality per-zone rather than one global model (ties FedVAR zonal normal,
  Mondrian-per-zone conformal, VACE positional manifold).
- **Calibration is reported as a FIRST-CLASS metric (Brier score), not just AUC/recall** — matches our
  UQ-first spine (T5). EF reduces Brier in BOTH regions: a reminder that fusion choice affects
  CALIBRATION, not just discrimination. We should report Brier/CRPS per-zone, as they do.
- **Honest reading of the F1 numbers:** landslide F1 is LOW (0.559 Nepal, worse than flood 0.722
  Kerala) even with the best model — a sober datapoint that **landslide susceptibility is intrinsically
  harder/noisier than flood**, consistent with Thread J (onset/precursor hard) and Rule 16 (no
  overclaiming). Good calibrated recall (0.901) with modest F1 means many false positives — exactly the
  cost-sensitive-threshold regime our Bayes p*=0.0476 + cost-loss framing is built for.
