## Content (own words)
- Problem: standard forecast-evaluation metrics (log score, etc.) do NOT account for the COSTS and
  LOSSES the decision-maker actually faces, so a model that "scores well" may not be the most
  USEFUL to act on.
- Method: adapt the classical meteorological COST-LOSS decision framework (Thompson/Murphy;
  Epstein 1969) to epidemiology. Assign each model a VALUE SCORE (VS) = compare the expected
  EXPENSE of a decision-maker ACTING on the model's forecast vs the expected expense of acting on
  the historical/climatological base-rate probability. VS depends on the COST-LOSS RATIO. VS > 0 =
  the model adds value over just using base rates; VS < 0 = base rates beat the model.
- Verified findings: applied to FluSight influenza-peak forecasts — most models have POSITIVE VS
  for SOME range of cost-loss ratios, but there is NO clear relationship between VS and the models'
  ranking under a (modified) log score. Reason: VS is SENSITIVE to over- vs under-prediction;
  standard scores are not.

## What it CHANGED in our thinking (required for quota)
- This is the capstone evaluation principle for TRINETRA and it SHARPENS several earlier threads:
  1. It gives a rigorous name + method for exactly our Bayes operating point: the cost-loss ratio
     IS C_FA/(C_FA+C_miss)=p*. The Value Score is the right top-line EWS metric — "does acting on
     TRINETRA's alarm cost less than acting on climatology/base-rate?" — not AUC/F1.
  2. CRITICAL methodological warning: model RANKING by a statistical score (log score, AUC,
     Brier) does NOT predict ranking by decision VALUE. So a model that "wins" on F1 (already
     suspect per 2206.09981) can be WORSE for the decision-maker. ACTION: mandate that the §P1
     evaluation spec reports a Value-Score-style, cost-loss-indexed metric as the PRIMARY EWS
     criterion, with statistical scores as secondary diagnostics only.
  3. The "compare against CLIMATOLOGY/base rate" benchmark is the honest skill baseline — mirrors
     2608.12422's persistence/gradient-boosted baselines (CR-02): a deep model must beat the
     base-rate decision, evaluated at the operational cost-loss ratio, or it adds no value.
  4. VS sensitivity to over/under-prediction is exactly our asymmetry: under-prediction (missed
     hazard) is catastrophic; the metric must penalize it asymmetrically — consistent with
     miss-averse reward (miss=−100) and Rule 19.
- Together with DataCOPE (2311.14110, can we even evaluate the policy?), EnbPI (2010.09107,
  coverage), ProCal (2306.04590, calibration), and 2606.21130 (threshold on recall/false-alarm),
  this completes a coherent, honest EVALUATION STACK for the warning/decision layer:
  feasibility → calibration → coverage → threshold → decision-value, each benchmarked against a
  base-rate/climatology skill reference.
