## Content (own words)
- Problem: inverse problems in granular flows (landslide/debris-flow) — recover material
  params (e.g. friction angle phi) or boundary conditions from a target runout profile.
  High-fidelity simulators (DEM, MPM) are expensive AND non-differentiable → gradient-based
  optimization impossible; ML surrogates are differentiable but generalize poorly.
- Core idea (re-implementable): a DIFFERENTIABLE Graph Neural Network Simulator (GNS):
  represent the granular system as a graph, predict next-step evolution; use reverse-mode
  automatic differentiation (AD) through the GNS to get exact gradients for gradient-based
  inverse optimization. GNS generalizes beyond training data for optimization, and is orders
  of magnitude faster than finite-difference (FD) gradient approaches.
- Evidence seen (Table 2): GNS recovers friction angle phi from target runout; FD-based
  gradient underestimates (e.g. stuck near 36°–39°) because of the finite Δφ=0.05 step error
  O(Δφ), whereas AD gives accurate gradients → GNS+AD converges better. Demonstrated on
  single + multi-parameter (incl. baffle-placement design to limit runout).
