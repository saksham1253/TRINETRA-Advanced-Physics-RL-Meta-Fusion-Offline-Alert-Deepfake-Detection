## Content (own words)
- Problem: landslides are hard to predict (many spatially/temporally varying factors). ANNs
  improve accuracy but are UNINTERPRETABLE; physical/statistical models are interpretable but
  less accurate.
- Core idea (re-implementable): Superposable Neural Network (SNN) — an ADDITIVE ANN
  optimization framework (only TWO hidden layers) that is fully interpretable, high-accuracy,
  high-generalizability, low-complexity. Includes dataset-division + outcome-interpretation
  techniques. Additive structure → you can read off each factor's contribution.
- Validation + findings (seen in abstract/intro): trained on landslide inventories from
  THREE EASTERNMOST HIMALAYA regions. SNN OUTPERFORMED physically-based + statistical models
  and MATCHED state-of-the-art DNNs despite being tiny. Key interpretable finding: the PRODUCT
  of slope × precipitation, and hillslope ASPECT, are primary contributors — evidence of
  strong slope–climate coupling + microclimate effects on landslides.
