## Problem & why it matters
Conformal PID (proportional-integral-derivative) calibration gives valid long-run coverage for
time-series prediction intervals, but a single PID configuration trades coverage vs interval WIDTH
and no fixed config is best across regimes — and local coverage degrades right after abrupt
distribution shifts. For TRINETRA this is the exact window we care about (a hazard onset IS an
abrupt shift) and ties directly to CR-04 (coverage is approximate + shift-sensitive).

## Core idea / method (re-implementable sketch)
- Maintain a LIBRARY of M PID calibrators, each evolving its own threshold q^(m) under its own
  coverage feedback.
- Each step: compute a coverage–efficiency loss per expert (penalizes miscoverage AND normalized
  interval width), update online aggregation weights w^(m) (expert-aggregation / prediction-with-
  expert-advice style), with a shared multiplier that adapts the miscoverage penalty using feedback
  from the actually-reported interval.
- Aggregate thresholds: q_{t+1} = Σ_m w^(m)_{t+1} q^(m)_{t+1}; interval = ŷ ± q_{t+1}.
- Cost O(M) time + storage per round; expert updates parallelizable (Algorithm 1).

## Theory / guarantees (as stated)
- Local regret bounds for the weighted expert losses under the realized multiplier sequence.
- Pathwise upper bound on time-averaged aggregate miscoverage (control in expectation and a.s.
  under a stability condition). → long-run coverage retained while improving width.

## Key results (as stated; tables pruned)
- Across 4 simulation settings + 2 real-data applications, PID-Expert yields NARROWER mean intervals
  than a prespecified Conformal-PID benchmark while keeping empirical coverage near nominal.
- Beats expert-SELECTION and equal-averaging on the coverage–width trade-off.
- Rolling analysis: local coverage–efficiency trade-offs are WORST right after ABRUPT shifts
  (explicitly a distribution-shift simulation §5.1.2).

## Assumptions & limitations
- Stated: stability condition needed for a.s. coverage; marginal/long-run coverage, not conditional.
- Inferred: "near nominal overall" can still mean local under-coverage during the shift transient —
  i.e. in the first steps of a hazard onset, coverage can lag. Must label CLASS-B (empirical).
