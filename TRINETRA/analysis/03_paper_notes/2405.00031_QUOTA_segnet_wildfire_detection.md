## Content (own words)
- Problem: real-time wildfire detection from UAV/drone live imagery with LIMITED datasets; fire/
  smoke/water are "amorphous" objects (no fixed shape) → feature overload hurts CNN classifiers.
- Method: a "Segmented Neural Network" (SegNet) selection approach — reduce feature maps by
  segmentation to cut irrelevant features, boosting both speed and accuracy; emphasizes PIXEL
  DENSITY (don't shrink images too much or early-fire detail is lost) and careful filter-count
  selection. Evaluated on a real drone-flight dataset vs SOTA.
- Verified numbers (architecture search I saw): 5 conv + 1 dense → 54.71% acc; 11 conv + 3 dense
  → 39.31% acc (MORE layers HURT — overfit/feature overload); OPTIMAL 5 conv + 2 dense →
  98.18% acc at 240.375 ms/frame.

## What it CHANGED in our thinking (required for quota)

- The "amorphous object" + pixel-density point maps onto our flood/debris-flow imagery: aggressive
