## What it is
A resilient emergency-comms architecture that **reuses dense smart-city IoT** after a disaster:
- **Wi-Fi HaLow (IEEE 802.11ah) + 802.11s mesh** backbone to exchange text/location/image/audio/video
  between citizens and authorities when normal networks fail.
- **SDN** control plane for remote monitoring/config, carried over a **LoRa controlled-flooding
  mesh** as the low-bandwidth management/signaling channel.
- Real urban prototype (indoor + outdoor).

## Numbers (as-published, real test)
- Wi-Fi HaLow: avg latency **15–54.8 ms**; upload **134–726 Kbps**, download **117–682 Kbps**
  (degraded by obstacles / no line-of-sight / terrain slopes but remained stable).
- **LoRa control mesh: 94.96% average message success rate.**

## Why it matters to TRINETRA (WS7 offline-first comms + WS3 last-mile)
- **Direct architectural analog for TRINETRA's offline-first communication layer.** Our premise is
  that in a Himalayan disaster the cellular network may be down; HaLert validates a concrete,
  tested-in-field design: **HaLow mesh for richer data + LoRa for resilient low-rate signaling/
  alerts**, with ~95% LoRa delivery even in bad conditions.
- Concrete datapoints to anchor WS7 feasibility: LoRa message success ~95% in a real obstacle-rich
  urban test; HaLow latency tens of ms. These are the kind of VERIFIED numbers we can cite for the
  comms-layer design (not our own claims — Rule 16).
- **Two-tier comms doctrine for TRINETRA:** separate the HIGH-rate data path (HaLow/Wi-Fi mesh, when
  available) from the ALWAYS-ON low-rate alert/telemetry path (LoRa controlled flooding). The alert
  must survive on the LoRa tier alone. Pairs with 2604.24404 (authenticate alerts) and 2610.06241
  (edge intelligence) → the WS7 node design.
- Terrain-slope degradation explicitly measured → realistic caution for Himalayan deployment: expect
  worse than urban; budget link margin and multi-hop. Add to RISKS_FOR_BUILD.
