## What it is
A **passive, low-cost water-level tracking scheme** that uses ONLY **LTE downlink power metrics**
(per-antenna RSRP/RSSI/RSRQ) reported by COMMODITY receivers — no dedicated water sensor at all.
- Motivation (quoted): fixed instruments (acoustic/radar/camera/pressure probes) are "costly to install
  and maintain and are **vulnerable during extreme events**" — exactly TRINETRA's survivability concern.
- **Physics:** two-ray channel model → received power varies with surface height h as
  p₀(b)=A+B·cos(k·h(b)); the first-order term p₁(b)=−Bk·h′(b)·sin(k·h(b)) carries the RATE of change.
- **Method:** continuous wavelet transform (CWT) on RSRP isolates the semidiurnal tide band → a
  "summed-coefficient" signature that marks tide-turn times AND tracks tide-RATE (flow speed); these
  wavelet features guide a LIGHTWEIGHT neural net that learns level change from a short training segment.
- **Multi-base-station cooperative mode:** per-carrier CWTs fused by ROBUST MEDIAN → one tide-band
  feature, improving stability + resilience to local disturbances.
- **Results (as published):** 420 m river path, LoS → **0.8 cm RMSE / 0.5 cm MAE**; NLoS (vegetation +
  vessel traffic) → model TRANSFERS after brief fine-tuning → **1.7 cm RMSE / 0.8 cm MAE**. No array
  calibration, runs on standard hardware.

## Why it matters to TRINETRA (new ultra-low-cost river modality + survivability + three recurring design rules)
- **A candidate sensing layer we had not catalogued:** passive RF water-level sensing complements the
  ₹2,000 River-Eye LSPIV camera node and the LoRa stage sensors. Its killer property for us is
  **survivability + zero dedicated infrastructure** — it rides on existing cellular signals, so it can
  keep reporting river rise when a physical probe/camera is washed out or buried (the Chamoli/Blatten
  failure mode). Flag as a RESEARCH candidate for the sensor trade study (not a commitment).
- **Reinforces THREE design rules already in our corpus:**
  1. **Wavelet / time-frequency features for time series** — same lesson as WinoTS (2609.39337): the
     CWT tide-band feature is what makes a *lightweight* NN work. Strengthens the WS8 "use wavelet
     views, not raw" rule across BOTH representation-learning and hand-feature settings.
  2. **Robust-median multi-sensor fusion** — the multi-base-station median fusion is a concrete,
     outlier-resistant fusion primitive for our multi-node river sensing (ties the GMF bounded-residual
     + Mondrian-per-zone design; median > mean under local disturbance/spoofing).
  3. **Physics-guided features beat black-box** — deriving features from the two-ray model (not raw
     power) echoes our WS1 physics-gate doctrine and PINN discipline (2610.04828): put the physics in
     the features/gates, let a small learner handle the residual.
- Honest-cost angle: "commodity receiver, standard hardware, no calibration" fits the ≤₹2,000/node
  envelope better than radar — worth a BOM sketch in P6.

## Caveats / honesty
- Demonstrated on TIDAL water-level (semidiurnal, quasi-periodic) over a 420 m river path; a flash-flood
  / cloudburst river surge is NON-periodic and FAST — the semidiurnal-band wavelet feature would NOT
  directly transfer; the physics (power vs height) and the lightweight-learner-on-wavelet-features
  PATTERN transfer, the tide-specific feature does not. Must be re-validated for impulsive surge before
  any use; cm-accuracy claims are for the tidal LoS/NLoS experiments only.
- Needs LTE coverage + a base station across the water path (geometry-dependent) — Himalayan gorges may
  lack LoS cellular; treat as opportunistic augmentation, never a primary gate (Rule 19).
- Field study, single river; eess.SP preprint.
