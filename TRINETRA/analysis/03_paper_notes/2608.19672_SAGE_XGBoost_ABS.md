## Content (own words — abstract only)
- Problem: natural-hazard susceptibility mapping is constrained by LIMITED LABELED DATA, which hurts
  conventional ML generalization AND makes complex deep-learning models inapplicable.
- Method: SAGE (Spatially Augmented Graph Embeddings) — a structurally-informed FEATURE-ENGINEERING
  framework: controlled noise-based data augmentation + neighborhood-based graph embeddings. A
  K-nearest-neighbor graph derives local spatial statistics → PCA-reduced → combined with environmental
  covariates + spatial coordinates → fed to **XGBoost** = SAGE-XGBoost.
- Evaluation: landslide AND wildfire susceptibility. Reported (ABSTRACT, UNVERIFIED — D-008):
  - Outperforms conventional + spatially-explicit ML; **+33 percentage points absolute over Spatial
    XGBoost** across the two case studies.
  - AUC ≈ **0.97 landslide**, **0.95 wildfire**.
  - Feature-importance confirms graph embeddings help; improves spatial coherence, reduces local noise.
- Framing: "an efficient and TRANSFERABLE alternative to DEEP representation learning" for hazard
  assessment under limited supervision.
