## What it is
An **end-to-end automated DL pipeline for targeted monitoring of HIGH-RISK HIMALAYAN glacial lakes**
using **time-series Sentinel-1 SAR** — the single most TRINETRA-WS8-aligned paper in the foundation so
far. Key design choices:
- **"Temporal-first" training strategy:** instead of maximising SPATIAL coverage to train one
  generalistic model (the common approach), they train on a deep TIME-SERIES of a small cohort of
  specific high-risk lakes. Argues this is what EWS actually needs (track known dangerous lakes over
  time, not map all lakes once).
- **Cohort = 4 named lakes:** Tsho Rolpa, Chamlang Tsho, Tilicho, Gokyo (Nepal Himalaya) — real
  high-risk GLOF lakes.
- **Model:** U-Net with EfficientNet-B3 backbone. **Reported IoU = 0.9130** (lake-area segmentation).
- **SAR, not optical:** deliberately uses Sentinel-1 SAR because optical (Sentinel-2) is defeated by
  persistent Himalayan cloud — the exact cloud-penetration argument TRINETRA makes for SAR.
- **Operational engineering (rare + valuable):** Dockerised pipeline, automated ingestion via the ASF
  Search API, inference exposed via a RESTful endpoint. Explicitly shifts "static mapping → dynamic
  automated early warning."
- Longitudinal climatic analysis 2014–2025 of the lakes; §6 honest limitations.

## Why it matters to TRINETRA (near-perfect WS8 reference + an operational blueprint)
- **Direct WS8 architecture reference.** This is essentially a minimal version of our GLOF lake-area
  front-end: Sentinel-1 time series → U-Net segmentation → area-change tracking → EWS trigger. We can
  adopt the pipeline shape wholesale and extend it (physics gate, calibrated uncertainty, alarm policy).
- **"Temporal-first" validates our targeted-monitoring stance.** TRINETRA watches a KNOWN set of
  high-risk lakes/slopes in Uttarakhand+Himachal, not the whole Himalaya uniformly. This paper gives
  empirical backing that temporal-first on a curated high-risk cohort beats generalistic spatial-coverage
  training for EWS — a design decision we can now cite.
- **SAR-over-optical cloud argument confirmed by a GLOF-specific study** — reinforces WS1/WS8 reliance
  on Sentinel-1 and the fusion rationale (optical when clear, SAR always).
- **Operational Docker+API pattern** is a concrete, free, reproducible blueprint for our ingestion layer
  (ASF Search API for S1 is exactly what we'd use) — informs the "offline-first + cloud-ingest when
  connected" split.
- **The §6 limitation on spatial resolution is a GIFT for our design.** They note Sentinel-1 GRD at ~10m
  pixel but effective ~900 m² minimum detectable feature; day-to-day area change before a GLOF may be
  smaller than the detection floor. They flag that custom S1 BURST raw processing (10–20 m) would catch
  2.25–9× smaller changes, and that adding Sentinel-2/NISAR/Landsat → 1–2 day revisit for near-real-time.
  → **This directly scopes a known failure mode for our GLOF head** (precursor area-change may be
  sub-resolution) and points to the multimodal + burst-processing fix. Log to failure-mode catalogue.
- Named lakes (Tsho Rolpa etc.) give us a concrete validation cohort template for the Indian side
  (e.g. South Lhonak-type lakes — research note, cross-border caveat).

## Caveats / honesty
- **IoU 0.9130 is a SEGMENTATION (lake-area delineation) metric, NOT a GLOF-prediction/forecast metric.**
  Delineating the lake well ≠ predicting an outburst. This is a perception front-end; the hard
  forecasting problem (will it burst, when) is NOT solved here (same gap as Anantrasirichai 2005.03221
  and the 2608.12422 "WHEN is hard" finding). Do not conflate.
- Nepal lakes, 4-lake cohort — small sample; transfer to Indian Himalaya lakes is a HYPOTHESIS (Rule 16);
  temporal-first on 4 lakes risks overfitting to those basins' SAR signatures.
- Sentinel-1 resolution floor (~900 m² effective) means sub-resolution precursor changes are MISSED —
  an acknowledged, serious limitation for a lake that fails via a small breach; our design must not
  assume area-change is always observable at S1 GRD resolution (→ failure mode).
- Dockerised/REST pipeline needs connectivity + compute — contrast with our offline-first ₹2000-node
  constraint; the satellite-ingest layer can be cloud-side, but the edge alerting must survive without it.
