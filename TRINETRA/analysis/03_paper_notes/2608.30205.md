## What it is
**exPreCast-ENS** turns a *deterministic* 4 km radar nowcaster (exPreCast) into a **1 km PROBABILISTIC
ENSEMBLE** via a **conditional RESIDUAL diffusion** module that also **corrects systematic forecast
errors**. Abstract frames the motivation exactly as ours: "localized extreme precipitation is a major
trigger of urban flash floods AND landslides," and the hard problem is combining fine spatial detail WITH
probabilistic uncertainty.
- **Residual diffusion:** the diffusion model learns a residual/refinement conditioned on the deterministic
  backbone forecast — not a from-scratch generation. (Residual doctrine, 5th corroboration.)
- **Downscaling + bias correction in one:** 4 km → 1 km while fixing non-zero-mean bias and spatial
  redistribution of rainfall.
- **Probabilistic ensemble output** → native uncertainty, not a point forecast.
- Validated on **KMA** radar (Korea) + transferred to **MeteoNet** (France); event-level case studies on
  real flood-producing storms.

## Verified numbers (§2.3 conditional-recovery table, read directly)
Conditioned on the backbone (Base) and ground truth (GT), for the super-resolution refinement (SR):
- **Heavy-rain recovery rate** P(SR=1 | Base=0, GT=1) = **46.5% / 38.3%** (two settings) — i.e. the
  diffusion refinement RECOVERS 38–47% of heavy-rain pixels the deterministic backbone MISSED.
- **New false-alarm rate** P(SR=1 | Base=0, GT=0) = **0.73% / 0.45%** — recovery comes with very few new
  false alarms.
- **Hit-retention rate** P(SR=1 | Base=1, GT=1) = **94.5% / 94.7%** — preserves nearly all true detections.
- **Hit-loss rate** = **5.5% / 5.3%** — few true detections deleted.
This is a clean miss-recovery-vs-false-alarm trade characterization — directly our alarm-cost framing.

## Why it matters to TRINETRA
1. **WHEN-layer, hazard-core.** This is precipitation nowcasting for exactly our triggering mechanism
   (extreme localized rain → flash flood + landslide). The residual-diffusion refinement is a candidate
   design for turning a cheap deterministic IMERG/radar nowcast into a calibrated probabilistic field.
2. **5th independent corroboration of the residual doctrine** (after MW-Nowcast, DRUM, Nowcasting-Nets,
   TREA-Net): learn a *residual/refinement* on a deterministic physical backbone rather than forecasting
   from scratch. The pattern is now overwhelmingly supported across precip, disease, and EO forecasting
   → lock it into the GMF/forecast design as a first-class principle (Thread I).
3. **Probabilistic ensemble + bias correction** matches our T5 (uncertainty first-class) and the
   decision-oriented-UQ requirement from 2609.14821 (#40). exPreCast-ENS gives the *distribution*;
   2609.14821 tells us to *calibrate it to the decision loss*. The two compose into our WHEN→alarm stack.
4. **The conditional-recovery table is the right evaluation idiom for us.** Reporting recovery of
   backbone-missed heavy-rain pixels vs new-false-alarm rate is precisely the miss-cost/false-alarm-cost
   view of our Bayes alarm threshold. Adopt this verification style for the WHEN layer.
5. **Downscaling 4 km → 1 km** is relevant: IMERG is ~10 km; a residual-diffusion downscaler conditioned on
   coarse satellite precip + local radar/station could sharpen it toward catchment scale (HYPOTHESIS to
   test; our terrain is steep Himalaya, not flat urban Korea).

## Caveats / honesty
- **Domain = flat urban Korea/France radar; our terrain is steep Himalaya with sparse radar.** Transfer is
  a HYPOTHESIS (Rule 16); orographic precip + radar beam blockage in mountains are unaddressed here.
- Diffusion ensembles are COMPUTE-HEAVY (sampling cost); under our offline-edge + Kaggle budget this likely
  runs cloud-side, not on the ₹2000 node. Check inference cost before adopting.
- The recovery/false-alarm numbers are KMA-specific and threshold-dependent; they are evidence of the
  METHOD's behavior, not a performance promise for our data (Rule 16).
- "Diffusion refinement recovers missed heavy rain" is attractive but it also DELETES ~5.5% of true hits
  (hit-loss) — for life-safety, hit-loss on extreme events is the dangerous direction; any adoption must
  monitor hit-loss on the extreme tail specifically, not just aggregate skill.
