## What it is
A **"safety-by-construction"** alternative to runtime shielding: instead of filtering/penalizing an
unsafe action at runtime, safety is **embedded in the policy CLASS**. They build a **finite library
of feedback controllers sharing a common Lyapunov certificate** that establishes **forward
invariance** of a prescribed admissible (safe) set under **arbitrary switching**. Any policy that
only picks from this library — including during RL exploration — **inherits the certificate**. A DQN
then schedules among the certified controllers (quadcopter hover), optimizing performance WITHOUT
runtime safety filtering. Validated in MuJoCo under wind, model mismatch, sensor noise, sensing delay.
- Key inequality read directly: V̇ ≤ −(α/λ_max(P))·V + β‖r_d⁽⁴⁾(t)‖² — the common dissipation bound
  holds for every admissible controller and is preserved under switching.

## Why it matters to TRINETRA (WS5 alarm policy, CR-03)
- A THIRD design pattern for safety, alongside (a) hard physics overrides and (b) adaptive
  probabilistic shield (2608.19836): **restrict the policy to a library of pre-certified SAFE
  actions** so even an exploring/learned policy can't do something unsafe.
- For our alarm policy this maps cleanly: define the action set as a **finite set of alert levels +
  escalation moves that are each pre-vetted to be safe** (e.g., "never DOWNGRADE Red within N hours
  of a physics trigger"; "escalation is always allowed"). Then whatever the RL policy learns, it can
  only choose among safe moves → safety-by-construction, no runtime override needed for those.
- Complements the shield rather than replacing it: library bounds the *action space*; the shield/
  override handles *state-based* hard stops. Together they give defense-in-depth (Rule 19).
- "Separate safety certification from performance optimization" is a clean architectural principle to
  state explicitly in the WS5 design doc.
