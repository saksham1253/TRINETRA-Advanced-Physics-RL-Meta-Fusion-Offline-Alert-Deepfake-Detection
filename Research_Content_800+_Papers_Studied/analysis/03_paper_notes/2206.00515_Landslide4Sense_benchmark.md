## Content (own words)
- Contribution: Landslide4Sense, a public multi-source benchmark for landslide detection from
  remote sensing. 3,799 image patches, each FUSING Sentinel-2 optical bands + DEM + slope layer
  (DEM/slope from ALOS PALSAR). Adding TOPOGRAPHY (DEM+slope) sharpens landslide-border
  detection vs optical-alone. Pixel-level binary labels (landslide / not), manual annotation.
- Sites/times: Iburi (Sep 2018), Kodagu (Aug 2018), **Gorkha (Apr 2015 — Nepal Himalaya)**,
  Taiwan (Aug 2009). 4 locations × times.
- Benchmark: 11 SOTA segmentation models trained FROM SCRATCH — U-Net, ResU-Net, PSPNet,
  ContextNet, DeepLab-v2, DeepLab-v3+, FCN-8s, LinkNet, FRRN-A, FRRN-B, SQNet. Protocol: train
  on patches from ONE quarter of each study area, test on the OTHER THREE quarters (spatial
  holdout). Result: ResU-Net wins. Verified reference numbers: their lowest result ≈ 56.4% F1
  (PSPNet); a cited transferability study got 73.32% F1 (ResU-Net) cross-dataset.
- Public: data + models at iarai.ac.at/landslide4sense.

## Relevance to us
- Component: WS1/WS8 satellite landslide layer — HIGH. (1) DATASET: a labeled, public,
  multi-source (optical+DEM+slope) landslide-segmentation benchmark INCLUDING Himalaya (Gorkha)
  → strong candidate for DATASETS.md (verify license at IARAI). (2) DESIGN: validates FUSING
  topography with optical — matches TRINETRA's physics-informed features (slope drives FS). 
  (3) EVALUATION: their spatial-quarter holdout is the right instinct but WEAKER than full
  tile-withheld spatial-CV (train/test quarters of the SAME event still share spatial
  autocorrelation) → our protocol must be stricter (ties CR-02 / 2608.12422 doctrine).
- Honesty caveat: F1 ~56–73% on this benchmark shows landslide SEGMENTATION is HARD even for
  tuned DL — a realism anchor against over-optimistic detection claims (Rule 16). These are
  THEIR numbers, not ours.
- Transfer hypothesis: Gorkha (Nepal) ≈ our Uttarakhand/Himachal terrain → good transfer for
  pretraining/validation; still validate per-region. confidence: high (dataset+protocol seen).
