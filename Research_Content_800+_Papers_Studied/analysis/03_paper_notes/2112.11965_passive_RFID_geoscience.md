## Content (own words, from abstract)
- Scope: reviews passive RFID for geoscience monitoring. Passive tags cost 10–100× LESS than
  wireless-sensor-network nodes and need little maintenance → enable years-long monitoring with
  tens to thousands of tags.
- Demonstrated applications: most mature = coarse SEDIMENT TRANSPORT in rivers/coasts (tags in
  pebbles). More recent: LANDSLIDE DISPLACEMENT monitoring via tag localization at CENTIMETRIC
  accuracy; displacement-THRESHOLD detection on unstable rocks; soil moisture/temperature;
  snowpack temperature + snow-water-equivalent. Potential: structure vibration, boulder tilt,
  material strain, water salinity.
- Key challenges: using ground/aerial vehicles to collect/localize tags; increasing read range
  + duration; reading tags under ground/snow/water/vegetation; optimizing economic +
  environmental cost. Positioning: passive RFID fills the gap between WSNs and manual
  measurement — efficient, high-spatial-density, multi-year, moderate cost.

## Relevance to us
- Component: WS7 (ground sensing) — a potentially TRANSFORMATIVE ultra-low-cost layer for
  TRINETRA. If passive RFID tags give centimetric LANDSLIDE DISPLACEMENT + boulder-TILT +
  snowpack/soil-moisture sensing at 10–100× lower cost than active LoRa nodes, that directly
  serves the ≤₹2000/node budget and lets us instrument FAR MORE slope points per rupee
  (dense spatial coverage — exactly what 2311.01564 El-Forn showed improves estimation).
  Complements (not replaces) the active LoRa/River-Eye nodes: tags are passive/cheap but need
  a reader to pass by (vehicle/drone/fixed interrogator) → data latency, which conflicts with
  REAL-TIME early warning. So: candidate for slow-variable baselining (displacement trend,
  snowpack), NOT for instantaneous alarms.
- Caution (honesty): read-range + under-cover reading + needing a mobile reader are real limits
  for an always-on EWS; this is a HYPOTHESIS layer, not a committed design. Abstract-only — do
  not assert the cm-accuracy for our setting without reading the journal version.
