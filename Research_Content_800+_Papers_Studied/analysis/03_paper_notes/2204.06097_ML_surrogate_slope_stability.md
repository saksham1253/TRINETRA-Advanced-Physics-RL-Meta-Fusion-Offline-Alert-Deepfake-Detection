## Content (own words, from abstract)
- Problem: random-field Monte-Carlo (MC) reliability analysis gives a robust probability of
  slope failure but needs MANY expensive numerical simulations.
- Core idea: train ML SURROGATE models on a SMALL number of random-field slope-stability
  simulations to predict the outcome (failure / non-failure) of a huge dataset, skipping the
  slow factor-of-safety calculation (binary failure examination only).
- Evidence (verified numbers from abstract): dataset = 120,000 finite-difference MC slope-
  stability simulations across soil heterogeneity + anisotropy levels. Best models among 9:
  Bagging Ensemble, Random Forest, SVM. Trained on only 0.47% (500 samples), ML classifies all
  120,000 at 85% accuracy / 0.91 AUC. Performance DEGRADES with higher soil anisotropy +
  heterogeneity. ML-assisted MC: using 5% of MC data, average error in predicted probability
  of failure is only 0.46%. Compute cut from 306 days → <6 hours.
