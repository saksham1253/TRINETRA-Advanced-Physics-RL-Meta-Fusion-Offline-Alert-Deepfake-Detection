## What it is
Addresses a real gap in shielded RL: shields are synthesized by rigorous formal methods and ENFORCE
safety, but their decisions are **opaque** — and because shields are non-deterministic, naive
decision-tree representations blow up and are not explainable. The paper makes the SAFETY layer
human-interpretable:
- Represents the shielding policy as a **HIERARCHY of decision trees** giving top-down, case-based
  explanations.
- **At design time:** a world model analyzes safety risk of each action in each state; builds the shield
  AND a high-level decision tree classifying states into **risk categories — safe / critical /
  dangerous / unsafe** — explaining WHY a situation is safety-critical.
- **At runtime:** generates LOCALIZED decision trees explaining which actions are allowed and why others
  are deemed unsafe.
- Uses **probabilistic safety guarantees** (Jansen et al. style world model), not just worst-case.
- Properties: no extra info beyond what shielding already uses; minimal overhead; drops into existing
  shielded-RL pipelines; explanation trees are "several orders of magnitude smaller" than the shield.
- Evaluated on Frozen Lake, Highway Cruise Control, Boeing Taxinet.

## Why it matters to TRINETRA (makes our hard-override shield AUDITABLE — a Rule-19 requirement, not a nice-to-have)
- **Our alarm-policy design already mandates a safety shield that hard-overrides the RL policy
  (Alshiekh 1708.08611). This paper makes that shield EXPLAINABLE, which Rule 19 (human oversight,
  complement certified systems) effectively requires for a life-safety alert system.** When TRINETRA's
  shield forces (or suppresses) an alarm, the district authority / NDMA officer must be told WHY in
  human terms. A hierarchy of small decision trees classifying the current basin state into
  safe/critical/dangerous/unsafe + a localized "these actions allowed, these forbidden because…" is
  exactly the audit artifact our gate messages and incident reviews need.
- **Risk-category state classification (safe/critical/dangerous/unsafe)** maps cleanly onto our
  CAP/SACHET alert levels (green/yellow/orange/red) — we can make the shield's risk category the
  explainable justification attached to each issued alert level, giving responders a formal,
  reproducible rationale rather than a black-box score.
- **Probabilistic (not only worst-case) safety guarantees** fit our regime: hazards are stochastic, so a
  probabilistic shield (bound violation probability) is more realistic than hard worst-case — ties the
  adaptive-probabilistic-shield read (2608.19836) and robust-shield (2606.00270, queued). Gives us a
  guarantee family to trade-study for the shield layer.
- **Minimal overhead + drops into existing shielded pipelines** → Kaggle-budget compatible; the
  explanation layer is cheap and offline-synthesized, so it does not burden the edge.
- Reinforces the project's broader "auditable, not just accurate" posture (ties CR-02's
  attribution-by-deletion and FL-MHSM's gating transparency): the DECISION layer, like the perception
  layer, must be inspectable before it touches a real alarm.

## Caveats / honesty
- Requires a WORLD MODEL (MDP + transition probabilities) to synthesize the shield and risk categories —
  TRINETRA's basin dynamics are only partially known; our "world model" would be the physics gate
  (TRIGRS/infinite-slope + hydrology) with large parameter uncertainty, so the probabilistic guarantee
  is only as good as that model (document as assumption; the shield certifies against the MODEL, not
  reality — same honesty caveat as all model-based safety).
- Benchmarks are small/clean (Frozen Lake, Highway, Taxinet) — scaling the hierarchical-explanation
  construction to a large continuous basin-state space is unproven; may need state abstraction.
- Explanation faithfulness: the small trees are approximations of a non-deterministic shield — must
  verify the explanation never understates danger (an explanation that wrongly reassures is a
  life-safety failure; pair with conservative default).
