## Content (own words)
- Problem: in rare-event (highly class-imbalanced) problems, performance METRICS can "misjudge" a
  model because of the skew, giving overly optimistic/distorted results. The community knows WHICH
  metrics are imbalance-sensitive, but NOT the MAGNITUDE of that sensitivity — so people either
  wrongly dismiss a useful metric or wrongly trust a distorted one.
- Method: an evaluation FRAMEWORK that QUANTIFIES a metric's sensitivity to class imbalance. Built
  on a "base contingency space" (inspired by ROC space) where each point is a family of
  equivalent confusion matrices; measures how a metric's value moves purely as the imbalance ratio
  changes, holding model behaviour fixed. Insists the test set must preserve the TRUE scarcity
  (not be rebalanced), or metric values become incomparable.
- Verified finding: there is a LOGARITHMIC behaviour in metric sensitivity — higher imbalance
  ratios are associated with LOWER marginal sensitivity. KEY practical warning: metric quantities
  are NOT directly comparable across different imbalance ratios (a common, under-emphasized error).

## What it CHANGED in our thinking (required for quota)
- Hardens TRINETRA's evaluation doctrine (CR-02 / FM-01 / FM-12 / the §P1 evaluation deliverable)
  with a precise rule we did not have: F1/precision/recall/accuracy values computed at ONE
  imbalance ratio CANNOT be compared to values at another ratio. For us this is acute: landslide/
  GLOF positives are vanishingly rare, and the imbalance ratio DIFFERS across zones, seasons, and
  between train vs deployment. So a model that "improves F1" on a rebalanced training fold may not
  improve anything in deployment, and cross-zone metric comparisons are invalid unless the
  imbalance ratio is reported and controlled. ACTION: mandate in the evaluation spec that every
  reported metric is accompanied by the imbalance ratio, that test folds preserve true base rates
  (no test-time rebalancing), and that cross-setting comparisons are only made at matched ratios
  or via imbalance-robust metrics.
- Reinforces preferring metrics tied to the DECISION/cost (cost-weighted, the Bayes threshold
  p*=0.0476 framing, precision-recall over ROC) rather than raw accuracy — and explains WHY
  Landslide4Sense F1 of 56-73% (2206.00515) must be read in light of its base rate, not taken at
  face value.
- Directly supports Bug #1 / threshold-consistency work: thresholds and the metrics used to tune
  them must be defined against the true operational base rate, not a convenient balanced set.

## Honesty caveats
- Their framework is generic/synthetic contingency analysis, not hazard data; the logarithmic law
  is their result (Rule 16), to be applied as a methodological caution, not a transferred number.
  Per-metric sensitivity tables unread. confidence: high — this is methodology, and the "don't
  compare metrics across imbalance ratios" rule is robust and directly actionable.
