## Content (own words)
- Problem: compound floods (surge + tide + rain + river discharge together) exceed any single
  driver; hydrodynamic models are accurate but too expensive for real-time; pure ML is fast
  but physically inconsistent and unrealistic at extremes.
- Core idea (re-implementable): ALPINE = a PINN enforcing the FULL shallow-water equations
  (mass conservation + BOTH momentum equations) — not partial constraints. Architecture:
  convolutional encoder-decoder + ConvLSTM temporal processing. Composite loss = data fidelity
  + physics residuals. Training stability for long autoregressive rollouts: scheduled sampling
  (gradually feed model's own predictions), small Gaussian noise on history channels during
  training only, length-8 backprop penalizing multi-step drift; open-loop at inference.
- Data: six historical storm events (4 train / 1 val / 1 held-out test). Claims substantial
  improvement over baseline NNs on water-surface elevation + velocity; physics constraints
  most valuable at PEAK storm intensity (when it matters most).

## Relevance to us
- Component: WS1 (physics gate) + WS3 (flash-flood/compound). Takeaway: concrete template for
  a PHYSICS-INFORMED emulator that stays realistic at extremes — directly relevant to the
  flash-flood/cloudburst layer and to our "physics gate" design (hard shallow-water/mass
  constraints as a sanity filter on learned predictions). The stability tricks (scheduled
  sampling + noise + bounded BPTT) are reusable for any autoregressive hazard forecaster.
- Transfer hypothesis: coastal compound flooding ≠ Himalayan flash-flood/GLOF hydraulics
  (steep channels, debris); shallow-water enforcement still applies but geometry/roughness
  differ. Validate on mountain-channel data. CLASS A (conservation laws) once labeled in P7.
- confidence: high on method; med on direct Himalayan transfer.
