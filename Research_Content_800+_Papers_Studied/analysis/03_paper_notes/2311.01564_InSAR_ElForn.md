## Content (own words)
- Problem: borehole monitoring of deep-seated landslides is expensive/labor-intensive; how
  faithful is InSAR as a substitute/complement, and how many in-situ sensors are really needed?
- Core idea (re-implementable): build a Sentinel-1 velocity map from 2019–2021 deformation
  time series for the in-situ-monitored El Forn landslide (Canillo, Andorra); compare TWO
  InSAR sources — EGMS (European Ground Motion Service) vs ASF On-Demand (HyP3) — against
  borehole displacement in the landslide-motion direction. Then use geospatial interpolation
  (ordinary KRIGING) + statistics to find how many in-situ observations minimize error of an
  InSAR-based ground-motion reconstruction.
- Key quantified finding (seen in abstract): ~20–25 total in-situ observations give the
  optimal normalized RMSE for an ordinarily-kriged model of the El Forn surface; InSAR +
  sparse field monitoring together give optimal info with minimum deployment.

## Relevance to us
