## What it is
The SEMINAL proof-of-concept for applying deep learning to **national-scale Sentinel-1 InSAR velocity
fields** to detect & classify ground deformation (coal-mining subsidence, groundwater
withdrawal/dewatering uplift, LANDSLIDES, tunnelling) — moving beyond unreliable thresholding so
non-expert stakeholders get usable information. Adapts a PRE-TRAINED CNN (transfer learning). Confronts
three problems that are IDENTICAL to TRINETRA's InSAR constraints and proposes a fix for each:
1. **Sparsity of measurement points + spike noise** → **spatial interpolation via modified MATRIX
   COMPLETION** (beats Delaunay triangulation) to make convolution well-posed on sparse PS/velocity
   fields.
2. **Insufficient ground-truth for a balanced training set** → **SYNTHETIC training dataset** built from
   the statistical characteristics of the real UK velocity map (synthetic-deformation-on-real-noise —
   the same strategy WaveDINO later uses).
3. **Wrapped-phase ambiguity** → **enhanced over-wrapping** techniques.
- **Ascending/descending fusion:** processes each LOS pass independently and averages probability maps
  (noise causing a false positive in one pass often absent in another); with 4 looks (2 asc + 2 desc),
  final map = max of the four asc–desc pair averages.
- Validated on UK velocity maps 2015–2019; detects real subsidence/uplift/landslide/quarry/tunnelling
  signals.

## Why it matters to TRINETRA (the methodological bedrock of our InSAR front-end, and the source of three reusable tricks)
- **This is the origin paper for the exact InSAR-DL lineage TRINETRA builds on** (same Bristol group →
  WaveDINO 2606.16795, Rouet-Leduc denoising 2012.13849 is the complementary ENS/LANL line). Reading it
  grounds our WS1 design decisions in the primary source, not just the recent descendants.
- **All three enhancements are directly transferable to Himalayan InSAR**, which is EVEN sparser/noisier
  than the UK (steep terrain, layover, shadow, decorrelation over vegetation/snow):
  - **Matrix-completion interpolation** → how we densify sparse PS/DS points before feeding the SSL
    encoder; a concrete pre-processing step for the pipeline.
  - **Synthetic-training-from-real-statistics** → our answer to scarce Himalayan deformation labels;
    build synthetic slow-slip/pre-failure signals on top of real Himalayan noise statistics (ties the
    SSL masked-modeling plan + the labquake-AE weak-precursor thread J).
  - **Over-wrapping / wrapped-phase handling** → informs whether we work in wrapped or unwrapped domain
    (WaveDINO works unwrapped + atmospheric-corrected; this is the earlier wrapped-domain approach).
- **Ascending/descending multi-look probability averaging + max-of-pairs** is a cheap, robust
  FALSE-POSITIVE suppressor we can adopt verbatim for the InSAR anomaly layer — a design-level
  false-alarm control that costs nothing and exploits geometry diversity (complements our FA-cost
  weighting; a free precision gain feeding the physics gate).
- **"Slower and more localised deformation signals" + sparse data** = exactly the pre-failure
  slow-slip regime we care about (vs fast volcanic/coseismic) — confirms that landslide precursors are
  the HARD end of the InSAR-DL difficulty spectrum and that aggressive denoising/interpolation +
  synthetic data are prerequisites, not optional (reinforces T2 "inputs are the weak link").

## Caveats / honesty
- 2020 CNN proof-of-concept — predates foundation models / transformers; the ARCHITECTURE is dated
  (adopt the DATA tricks, not the specific net). Transfer-learning from an ImageNet-pretrained CNN is
  weaker than modern EO-FM conditioning (CR-01).
- UK (gentle terrain, dense PS, pan-national service) ≠ Himalaya (steep, decorrelated, sparse) —
  matrix-completion + synthetic-stats must be re-fit to Himalayan velocity-field statistics; numbers do
  not transfer (Rule 16).
- Synthetic training data risks a sim-to-real gap if the synthetic signal statistics miss real
  pre-failure morphology — same caveat as WaveDINO; validate against any available real Himalayan
  deformation events (Joshimath 2023, etc. — research-note only).
- Detects/classifies deformation TYPE, does not FORECAST failure timing — it is a perception
  front-end, not an early-warning system (forecasting is the harder downstream problem, Thread J).
