## What it is
AFOCP upgrades **Online Conformal Prediction (OCP)** — which wraps ANY pre-trained predictor to give
coverage guarantees that hold DESPITE temporal dependence / distribution shift — by fixing two standard
OCP weaknesses:
1. **Feature-space calibration (FOCP):** compute nonconformity scores in the FEATURE space of the
   pre-trained net (learned representations) rather than raw output space → concentrates on
   task-relevant info, suppresses nuisance variation → more COMPACT prediction sets.
2. **Attention-based adaptive weighting:** an attention mechanism adaptively weights historical
   observations by relevance to the current test point (instead of treating all history uniformly) →
   handles non-stationarity / distribution shift.
- **Theory:** maintains long-term coverage AND provably achieves SMALLER intervals than standard OCP
  under mild regularity conditions. Clean special cases: AOCP (attention only), FOCP (feature only).
- **Results (as published):** on synthetic + real time series, reduces interval SIZE by **up to 88%**
  vs OCP at the SAME target coverage.

## Why it matters to TRINETRA (makes our conformal layer EFFICIENT without losing the guarantee — directly actionable for GMF)
- **This is the width-reducing complement to our existing conformal stack.** Our GMF already uses
  per-zone Mondrian conformal + EnbPI/DistMatch base + PID-Expert for width; AFOCP adds the key missing
  idea: **do conformal calibration in FEATURE space, not output space.** For our sensor streams, raw
  output-space intervals are wide because of nuisance variation (atmospheric noise, sensor jitter);
  calibrating in the learned feature space (e.g. the SSL InSAR encoder's latent, or the fused GMF
  embedding) should give MUCH tighter people-at-risk / lead-time intervals at the same coverage — a
  direct, theory-backed efficiency win. Up-to-88% narrower is the headline motivation (their datasets,
  not ours; Rule 16 — do not claim it for TRINETRA).
- **Attention over history = principled handling of non-stationarity**, which is exactly our regime
  (monsoon onset, regime shifts, seasonal InSAR). It downweights stale history after an abrupt shift —
  complements DistMatch's adaptive binning and PID-Expert's feedback, giving us THREE composable
  non-stationarity tools to trade-study for the conformal module.
- **Wrapper around ANY pre-trained predictor + coverage guarantee preserved** → fits our "UQ is
  first-class and must hold under shift" spine (T5) and the honesty requirement that intervals be
  VALID, not just narrow. The theoretical long-term-coverage guarantee is what lets us trust a tight
  interval feeding a life-safety alarm (Rule 19).
- Simeone-group rigor (information-theoretic) — a high-confidence methodological anchor; the special
  cases (AOCP, FOCP) give us an ablation ladder to adopt incrementally (start with FOCP, add attention).

## Caveats / honesty
- "Up to 88%" is a best-case over their datasets/metrics; typical reduction will be smaller and
  dataset-dependent — must re-measure on Himalayan sensor streams; do NOT quote 88% for us.
- Feature-space calibration quality DEPENDS on the pre-trained representation being good — if our SSL
  encoder underfits (scarce labels), the feature space may not help as much; interacts with CR-01
  encoder choice. Attention adds compute/latency on-device — check against edge budget (ties
  prototype-head on-device thread; AFOCP is heavier).
- Coverage guarantee is LONG-TERM / asymptotic under regularity — short-horizon local miscoverage can
  still occur right after an abrupt shift (they note local coverage–efficiency trade-offs), which for a
  rare hazard is the dangerous moment → pair with a conservative shield (never let a tight interval
  suppress a physics-gated alarm).
