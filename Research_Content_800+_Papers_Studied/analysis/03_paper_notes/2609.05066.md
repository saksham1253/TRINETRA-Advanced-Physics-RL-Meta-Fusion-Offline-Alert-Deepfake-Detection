## Problem & why it matters
Weakly-supervised VAD (video-level labels only — the realistic supervision regime for us) usually
uses RIGID hand-crafted temporal priors (fixed snippet windows) that cannot adapt to the huge
variation in anomaly DURATION (a fall vs a flood develop on very different timescales). This paper
makes the temporal granularity ADAPTIVE. Relevant to WS4: our hazards span seconds (debris surge)
to hours (rising water), so fixed-window scoring is a known weakness.

## Core idea / method (re-implementable sketch)
- Temporal Refinement Module (TRM): refines snippet features across multiple temporal scales.
- Event Segmentation Module (ESM): segments the video into variable-length EVENTS rather than fixed
  snippets → captures anomalies of differing durations.
- Adaptive Score Fuse: combines snippet-level and event-level anomaly scores adaptively; snippet-level
  scores S^s used as final predictions.
- MIL framework (video-level labels), standard I3D/feature backbone.

## Data / splits
- UCF-Crime (1,900 untrimmed videos, 13 anomaly types; 1,610 train video-level / 290 test frame-level)
  and XD-Violence (4,754 videos, 6 violence categories, heterogeneous devices — a cross-device
  robustness stressor). Metric: frame-level AUC (UCF), AP (XD).

## Key results (as stated; tables pruned)
- Reports SOTA or near-SOTA frame-AUC on UCF-Crime and AP on XD-Violence vs recent WSVAD baselines;
  ablation shows TRM + ESM + adaptive fusion each contribute.

## Assumptions & limitations
- In-dataset evaluation on UCF/XD; no cross-dataset generalization test (FM-01 / C8 caveat).
- Standard VAD metrics (frame-AUC) — known to be optimistic (ties 2206.09981, FM-21).
- Method adds modules → more compute; edge feasibility not addressed.
