## What it is
A deliberately **interpretable** video deepfake detector: instead of an end-to-end black box, it
extracts **explicit, named per-frame forensic features** in four families, then models their
**temporal evolution** with an LSTM.
- Photometric family (18 vars): per-channel means/std across RGB/HSV/YCrCb + colour ratios (eqs 5–6
  read directly).
- Textural family (24 vars), Geometric family (14 vars), Compression-related family (12 vars).
- Face tracking + identity-association cost to follow faces across frames.
- Reported strengths: cross-dataset generalization + ablations; interpretability is the selling point.

## Why it matters to TRINETRA (WS2 media forensics)
- WS2's job in TRINETRA is NOT general deepfake detection — it is **rejecting fabricated/forged
  imagery in the crowd/social-media + CCTV corroboration layer** (someone posting a fake "dam burst"
  video, or tampered CCTV). An **interpretable, feature-based** detector is far more defensible for a
  life-safety decision (Rule 19) than a black box: a human reviewer can see WHY a clip was flagged.
- **Design takeaway:** prefer explicit forensic features (photometric/compression inconsistency) +
  temporal consistency as a FIRST-PASS filter whose output a human can audit, reserving heavy models
  for escalation. Pairs with the P2 finding (2609.07455) that the crowd/social layer is low-weight
  and corroborating only.
- Compression-family features connect to our WS8 CCTV pipeline (re-encoded social video, same
  distortions the provenance benchmark 2610.04151 studies).
