## Problem & why it matters
A structured, recent reference on UQ METHODS (how to get uncertainty) and MEASURES (how to score
it) for deep learning, with an explicit PRACTICAL-LIMITATIONS subsection per method. This is the
backbone reference for TRINETRA's WS6 uncertainty layer: it lets us pick a UQ method that fits
Kaggle compute + edge constraints and know its failure modes.

## Core content (reusable taxonomy)
- Uncertainty split: ALEATORIC (irreducible data noise) vs EPISTEMIC (model/knowledge — reducible
  with data; the OOD-relevant kind).
- Method families + their practical limits:
  - Bayesian NNs (VI, MCMC, Laplace/linearized): principled but expensive / hard to scale; Laplace
    + last-layer is the cheap practical compromise.
  - MC Dropout: cheap approximate inference, but quality depends on dropout rate + often
    under-estimates epistemic uncertainty (limitation flagged).
  - Deep Ensembles: strongest empirical UQ, loss-landscape multi-basin averaging, but N× train +
    inference cost — borderline for edge.
  - Efficient ensembles: snapshot ensembles (2017), SWAG (2019), Sub-Ensembles, BatchEnsemble
    (rank-one perturbations, 2020), MultiSWAG, Masksembles (2021), Layer Ensembles (single pass),
    last-layer Bayes (fixes ReLU overconfidence far from data, 2020), DUQ (RBF distance to class
    centroids) — these are the compute-feasible options for us.

## Key takeaways (survey-level)
- No single UQ method dominates; Deep Ensembles are the empirical gold standard but costly;
  BatchEnsemble / last-layer Bayes / MC-Dropout are the realistic edge-feasible approximations.
- Epistemic uncertainty is the OOD-relevant signal but the hardest to estimate reliably (ties
  2402.09056 "epistemic only relative" caveat already in ledger).
