## What it is
A **structured survey** of DL anomaly detection for railway systems with an explicit, reusable
**unified taxonomy** and a **decision-oriented framework** mapping problem properties → method →
deployment config. This is the kind of foundational "surveys + seminal" anchor P3 explicitly wants.

## The reusable taxonomy (directly portable to TRINETRA WS8)
- **By location of occurrence** (where the anomaly lives: rolling stock vs infrastructure → for us:
  sensor node vs slope vs river vs regional).
- **By manifestation in data** (point / contextual / collective anomalies).
- **By sensing modality** (vibration, acoustic, vision, etc. → for us: InSAR, tilt, rain, river
  stage, CCTV).
- **By temporal behavior** (transient vs persistent; abrupt vs gradual — maps to our precursor
  dynamics: creep vs sudden failure).
- **Four learning paradigms:** classification-based, prediction/forecast-based, reconstruction-based,
  and hybrid. (Our stack spans forecast-residual = prediction-based + Deep SVDD = one-class
  classification + isolation forest + XGBoost = hybrid → the survey confirms we're using a sensible
  spread of paradigms.)
- **Deployment axis:** edge-cloud architectures, computational constraints, hardware-aware
  optimization — the survey treats deployment as first-class (most AD surveys don't). Good support
  for our edge-first WS7/WS8 framing.

## Why it matters to TRINETRA
- Gives us a **vetted vocabulary + decision framework** to structure the WS8 anomaly-layer design
  doc and the REFERENCES.md anomaly section — exactly a P3 deliverable need (grouped references +
  taxonomy).
- The "prediction-based paradigm" discussion connects to the NCL warning (2610.06453): reconstruction/
  prediction methods are a MAJOR paradigm, and NCL shows their failure under over-capable models →
  we cite both: use the paradigm, guard its failure mode.
- Reinforces our **multi-paradigm ensemble** choice as principled, not ad hoc.
- Railway = safety-critical monitoring with heterogeneous sensors + edge constraints = structurally
  close to a geohazard WSN; the deployment-decision framework transfers well.
