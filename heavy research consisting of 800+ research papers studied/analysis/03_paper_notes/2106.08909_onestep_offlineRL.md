## Content (own words)
- Problem: dominant deep offline-RL = iterative actor-critic that repeatedly does OFF-POLICY
  EVALUATION (OPE) to learn a critic; OPE is high-variance and that variance is magnified by
  repeated policy optimization against noisy Q estimates.
- Core idea (re-implementable): do just ONE STEP of constrained/regularized policy improvement
  using an ON-POLICY Q estimate of the BEHAVIOR policy (fit Q^β by SARSA-style on-policy
  evaluation of the data-generating policy, then improve once). No iterated DP, no OPE.
- Evidence seen (Table 2, reverse-KL, verified numbers): one-step BEATS multi-step and
  iterative on most mujoco-medium tasks — halfcheetah-m 55.6 vs 40.8 vs 47.4; walker2d-m 85.6
  vs 75.9 vs 75.4; hopper-m 83.3 vs 53.0 vs 54.2. Exception: RANDOM data (esp. halfcheetah),
  where ITERATIVE wins. Guidance: iterative helps when the dataset is LARGE with GOOD coverage
  (then OPE succeeds); otherwise one-step is simpler, more robust to hyperparameters.
- Diagnosis: iterative failures come from (1) distribution shift behavior→eval policy, (2)
  "iterative error exploitation" — DP propagates optimization-induced bias across states.

## Relevance to us
- Component: WS5 + CR-03 (multi-stage/iterative vs simple one-step offline RL). CRITICAL
  evidence for the SIMPLE side of CR-03: with our SMALL, narrow-coverage alarm dataset (few
  real disasters), OPE will be unreliable → one-step policy improvement is likely MORE robust
  and safer than CQL/IQL iteration. Strongly reinforces 2106.06860 (TD3+BC) parsimony lesson.
- Takeaway for design: default to one-step/simple offline improvement; only escalate to
  iterative CQL/IQL IF we can show large coverage (we probably cannot) — and always behind the
  safety shield (1708.08611).
- Transfer hypothesis: D4RL locomotion ≠ discrete alarm MDP; but the "OPE variance kills you
  on small/narrow data" argument transfers STRONGLY to our regime.
- confidence: HIGH (method + verified numbers). Directly updates CR-03 toward "prefer simple".
