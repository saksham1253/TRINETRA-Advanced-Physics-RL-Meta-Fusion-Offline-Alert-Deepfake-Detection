## Content (own words, from abstract)
- Problem: reliable high-res precipitation estimates are hard over HIGH MOUNTAINOUS complex
  terrain. The three measurement types — rain gauges, precip radar, satellite sensors — each
  have pros/cons. Data MERGING can reduce error. Study uses an LSTM to merge a radar-based
  product and the satellite GPM IMERG product at HOURLY scale; compares to MRMS reanalysis;
  validates against CDEC gauge observations (California).
- KEY FINDINGS (abstract-verbatim, important and CAUTIONARY):
  (1) The LSTM-merged precipitation NOTABLY UNDERESTIMATED gauge observations and sometimes
      FAILED to give meaningful estimates — "predominantly near-zero values."
  (2) Relying SOLELY on individual QPEs (quantitative precip estimates) WITHOUT additional
      meteorological input was INSUFFICIENT for a reliable merged QPE.
  (3) BUT the merged result captured TEMPORAL TRENDS well, outperforming MRMS on that axis.
  (4) Suggests BIAS-CORRECTION techniques could improve the merged product.
