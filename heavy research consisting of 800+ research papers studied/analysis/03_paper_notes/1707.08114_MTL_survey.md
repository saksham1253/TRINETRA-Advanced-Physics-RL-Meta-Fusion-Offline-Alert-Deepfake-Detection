## Content (own words)
- Problem/scope: survey of Multi-Task Learning (MTL) — jointly learning multiple related
  tasks so knowledge transfers between them, improving generalization and reducing per-task
  labeling needs (helps data-sparse tasks).
- Core taxonomy (5 categories): feature-learning, low-rank, task-clustering, task-relation-
  learning, decomposition approaches. MTL can combine with semi-supervised/active/unsup/RL/
  multi-view/graphical models. Reviews online/parallel/distributed MTL + dim-reduction/
  feature-hashing for scale, plus applications and theory.
- Distinctions clarified: MTL (all tasks matter) vs transfer learning (only target matters)
  vs multi-label/multi-output.

## Relevance to us
- Component: WS8 (multimodal/multi-hazard shared encoder) + methodology. Takeaway: TRINETRA
  predicts MULTIPLE related hazards (landslide / flash-flood / GLOF) from shared sensors —
  a natural MTL setting. Sharing a backbone across hazards could (a) help the data-sparsest
  hazard (GLOF, very few events — ties class-imbalance A8) via knowledge transfer, and (b)
  reduce compute (one encoder, several heads — fits Kaggle limits). The task-relation-learning
  category is relevant to deciding WHICH hazards should share parameters.
- Caution: negative transfer is real — forcing unrelated hazards to share can HURT; the
  survey's task-relation methods are the principled way to decide. Feeds the shared-vs-
  separate-head design question (Q4.x) and the GMF head.
- Transfer hypothesis: generic MTL theory; applicability to our tiny-GLOF-sample regime is a
  hypothesis to validate (P14). confidence: med-high on concepts; low on quantitative transfer.
