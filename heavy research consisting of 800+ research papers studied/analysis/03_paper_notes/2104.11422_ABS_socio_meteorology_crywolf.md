## Content (own words, from abstract)
- Builds a stylized SOCIO-HYDROLOGICAL model coupling: flood occurrence, social collective
  MEMORY, social collective TRUST in flood early-warning systems (FEWS), and preparedness
  actions taken in response to warnings — extending existing socio-hydrology models which did
  NOT explicitly simulate collective trust.
- Simulates the CRY-WOLF EFFECT: many false alarms undermine EWS credibility → harder to induce
  preparedness. Findings: (1) modelling trust dynamics matters MORE in a "technological society"
  with INFREQUENT floods than in a "green society" with frequent floods; (2) as physical forecast
  skill improves, FEWS efficiency becomes MORE sensitive to social trust → forecasters should set
  the WARNING THRESHOLD considering social aspects, not just physical skill.
