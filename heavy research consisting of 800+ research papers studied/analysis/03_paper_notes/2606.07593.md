## Problem & why it matters
Image classifiers in high-risk settings must survive input perturbations (blur, sharpen, noise).
Adversarial / corruption fine-tuning is the popular fix. This paper asks mechanistically what
adversarial fine-tuning actually does to a ViT and — crucially — whether robustness TRANSFERS to
corruption types not seen in training. The transfer question is the one that matters for TRINETRA:
we cannot enumerate every field corruption, so we need to know if training on some covers others.

## Core idea / method
- Adversarially fine-tune a ViT on LOW-frequency and HIGH-frequency image corruptions.
- Mechanistic probes: class-prediction progression across layers; attention-map analysis;
  representational analysis via sparse autoencoders (SAE) on internal activations.
- Compare performance + internal structure on seen vs unseen corruption classes.

## Key results (as stated)
- Fine-tuning on inputs with common corruptions improves accuracy + confidence on NEW INSTANCES of
  the SAME corruption class, BUT these gains DO NOT TRANSFER to other corruption classes not seen
  in training. (The central, load-bearing negative result.)
- Despite measurable changes in visual attention and layerwise "knowledge evolution", adversarial
  training did NOT fundamentally change the SPARSE representations the ViT learned — i.e. robustness
  gains are shallow / corruption-specific, not a deep representational shift.

## Assumptions & limitations
- Classification only, ViT only, specific corruption families. Preprint; some figures pruned.
- Does not test satellite/sensor streams — transfer to our modalities is a hypothesis.

## Reproducibility
- Standard ViT + corruption benchmarks; SAE tooling cited. No single headline metric (mechanistic study).
