## What it is
Combines **Federated Learning + TinyML compression** for **on-device intrusion detection** on
microcontroller-class IoT hardware (privacy-preserving: raw data never leaves the device). Addresses
the gap that conventional federated models are too large/unstable for MCUs, and TinyML is usually
inference-only (not trained in a federated loop).
- Evaluates compression WITHIN a federated training pipeline: **knowledge distillation + structured
  pruning + quantization**.
- **Key finding: TRAINING STABILITY is the critical lever.** Server-coordinated **cosine
  learning-rate scheduling** raises **Attack Recall from 46.7% → 93.85%** while still allowing
  substantial compression + efficient edge deployment.

## Why it matters to TRINETRA (WS7 node security + federated + the "stability matters" lesson)
- **Direct template for securing the TRINETRA LoRa/IoT node.** Our nodes are MCU-class, deployed in
  the field, on an open mesh (which 2604.24404 + RB-16 show is spoofable) → they need lightweight,
  privacy-preserving **intrusion/anomaly detection on-device**. This paper shows FL+TinyML can get
  there, and that **compression (KD+prune+quant) is compatible with federated training** — consistent
  with our prototype-head (2610.06241) and distillation (Prithvi→EfficientViT, 2609.20441) choices.
- **The headline lesson is methodological, not domain-specific:** in federated/compressed TinyML,
  **training STABILITY (LR schedule) dominates** — a bad schedule hid half the achievable recall
  (46.7 vs 93.85). This echoes 2610.03369 (RB-13: the "architecture fix" was really a training-spec
  fix). → Add to our build doctrine: **ablate the training recipe (LR schedule, exploration) before
  attributing performance to architecture or compression**; report stability across clients/seeds.
- Reinforces the **federated privacy thread** (with FedVAR 2608.06876 and federated VLM-verify
  2609.07455): TRINETRA can train shared detectors across many community-owned nodes/cameras without
  centralizing sensitive data — important for consent/law (expert review).
