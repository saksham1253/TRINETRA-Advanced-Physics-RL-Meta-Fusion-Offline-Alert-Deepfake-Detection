## What it is
PIER = **offline** RL for fuel-efficient, risk-aware ship routing learned from historical AIS +
ocean reanalysis in a **physics-calibrated environment**, with **NO online simulator/interaction**.
Validated on 2023 AIS, 7 Gulf-of-Mexico routes, 840 eval episodes/method. Uses **IQL** as the
offline-RL learner; adds **physics-informed state construction**, **demonstration-augmented offline
data**, and a **decoupled post-hoc safety shield**.

## Numbers (as-published, their protocol)
- Mean CO2 −10% vs great-circle; median savings only ~1% (mean masks the real story).
- **Primary contribution = killing the tail:** 95th-pct CO2 −6%, max single-voyage −70%; extreme
  fuel-waste voyages (>1.5× median) cut 4.8%→0.5% (9× fewer worst cases).
- Per-voyage variance 3.5× lower (p<0.001); bootstrap 95% CI mean savings [2.9%, 15.7%].
- **Forecast-independence:** classical A* path-opt's wave protection degrades 2.4× at σ=0.25 (48 h
  forecast noise) and 4.5× at σ=1.0; PIER stays constant because it uses only LOCAL observations.
  (A*'s arrival rate paradoxically *improves* under random forecasts by defaulting to short routes —
  a cautionary tale about reading one metric.)

## Why it matters to TRINETRA (WS5 alarm policy + evacuation)
- **Direct architecture analog to our WS5 design** and it is the strongest external validation yet
  of the exact stack we chose: offline RL (we picked CQL/IQL/TD3+BC) + a **decoupled post-hoc
  safety shield** (Alshiekh-style) + physics-informed state. The paper explicitly lists **wildfire
  evacuation** as a transfer target.
- **"Eliminate catastrophic tail, not improve the mean"** is exactly the right objective framing for
  a life-safety alarm policy: our reward already weights miss=−100; PIER confirms that reporting the
  TAIL (worst-case, 95th-pct, variance) matters more than the mean. ADD to WS5 eval: report
  worst-case-miss frequency + variance, not just mean reward/lead-time.
- **Forecast-independence doctrine (big one):** PIER beats a forecast-dependent optimizer precisely
  because operational forecasts are noisy. This reinforces our P2 finding (2605.17419: train for
  forecast-displacement; 2609.17175: ~90-min nowcast skill ceiling). Design rule: the alarm policy
  should degrade gracefully using LOCAL/current observations (tilt, pore pressure, river stage, InSAR
  rate) and NOT depend on a long-horizon forecast being correct.
- IQL (2110.06169, already in our stack) is the offline learner PIER settled on — supports our
  multi-stage CQL→IQL→TD3+BC plan.
