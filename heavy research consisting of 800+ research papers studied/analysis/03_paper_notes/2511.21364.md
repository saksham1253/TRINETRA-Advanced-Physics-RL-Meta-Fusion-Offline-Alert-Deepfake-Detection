## Problem & why it matters
Multimodal (text + image) disaster-type classification from SOCIAL MEDIA posts in BANGLA — a
low-resource South-Asian language and region (Bangladesh) adjacent to our target geography.
Relevant to TRINETRA's "third layer" of situational awareness (crowd-sourced/social reports as a
complementary, human-generated hazard signal) and to the low-resource-language reality of the
India/Himalaya context (Hindi/regional languages).

## Core idea / method (re-implementable sketch)
- New dataset: 5,037 Bangla social-media posts, each caption+image, 9 disaster categories.
- Text encoders compared: BanglaBERT, mBERT, XLM-RoBERTa. Visual backbones: ResNet50, DenseNet169,
  MobileNetV2.
- EARLY fusion of text + image features → classifier. Training config standard (reported inference
  0.45 s, 1.8 GB memory on standard GPU).

## Data / splits / generalization
- 5,037 posts, 9 classes, Bangla; single-source social-media dataset. No cross-region/cross-event
  held-out test mentioned → in-dataset evaluation (FM-01 caveat applies).

## Key results (as stated)
- Best multimodal (early fusion) = 83.76% accuracy; +3.84% over best text-only, +16.91% over best
  image-only → fusion helps, and TEXT is the stronger single modality here (image alone weak).
- Table V context: comparable English multimodal disaster systems reach F1 84–88% (CrisisSpot 88.1).
  Bangla text-only emergency binary tasks reach 95.3% (easier, 2-class).

## Assumptions & limitations
- Stated: low-resource language, 9-class ambiguity. 6-page workshop paper.
- Inferred: in-dataset only; social-media disaster labels are noisy + self-selected (reporting bias);
  image modality weak → fusion gain mostly from text. No calibration/uncertainty. No cross-event test.
