## What it is
A rigorous, honesty-first prediction TEST on **free satellite data alone** for **exactly TRINETRA's
hazard set in exactly our neighbourhood (Nepal Himalaya)**: large moraine-/ice-dam GLOF bursts,
rainfall-triggered landslides, and small glacier/pond floods. Design choices that make it the gold
standard to emulate:
- **Three hazards, two questions each, NEVER blended:** (WHICH site is susceptible) vs (WHEN a trigger
  arrives) — mirrors our deformation=where / weather=when decomposition.
- **589 dated outbursts (HMAGLOFDB) + several thousand catalogued landslides**, each matched against
  **similar-but-UNFAILED sites** (case-control; kills the base-rate illusion).
- **SPATIAL cross-validation withholding WHOLE MAP TILES** (block-keyed folds, not site-keyed) so no
  model can win by recognising a trained-on neighbour; **seeds fixed → reproducible to the digit**.
- **One simple gradient-boosted baseline + FIVE learned models** (SSL pretraining, label-free anomaly
  detection, attention, physics-informed, positive-unlabeled) — a full fair-comparison ladder.
- **Physics penalty:** PINN loss charges the model whenever ∂(hazard)/∂(slope) < 0 (hazard must not
  fall as a slope steepens) — physics as a soft monotonicity constraint.
- **Attribution by deletion** (feature-removal) to read what the winner actually uses.

### Results (as published, verified via spill)
- **R1 — WHEN (antecedent weather times the trigger):** ROC **0.73** big bursts, **0.83** landslides,
  **0.82** small floods. Weather is a genuine but modest timing signal.
- **R2 — WHERE (terrain susceptibility), the honesty bombshell:** scored NAIVELY terrain looks ~**0.9**;
  but matched against comparable nearby sites under spatial-CV the HONEST figures collapse to **0.76 /
  0.71 / 0.54** (small floods = no better than chance). The naive ~0.9 was largely a GEOGRAPHIC
  ARTIFACT (catalogued failures cluster in wetter ranges).
- **R3 — burst signal is real WITHIN a single region:** reaches **0.89 in Nepal alone**, depends on
  failure mechanism.
- **R4 — the headline for us:** **five deep-learning models do NOT decisively beat the simple
  gradient-boosted baseline.** Three score marginally higher on landslides — "a hint too small to
  confirm." **For the lake hazards the BASELINE WINS OUTRIGHT, reproduced by a THREE-RULE DECISION TREE
  on ruggedness + monsoon rainfall.**
- **R6 — deformation marks hazard but does NOT rank susceptibility** (InSAR tells you a dam is moving,
  not which lake is likelier to fail vs peers).
- Deliverable: a **ranked Nepal watchlist** — explicitly "a prioritisation AID, not a prediction."

## Why it matters to TRINETRA (the single most important external document for our honesty posture)
- **This is the empirical foundation of our entire evaluation doctrine, by independent authors on our
  exact problem.** It confirms, with numbers:
  1. **Naive (non-spatial) evaluation inflates AUC by ≈0.15–0.35** → quantifies our leakage bug (#11)
     and is the hard justification for our MANDATORY tile-withheld spatial-CV protocol.
  2. **DL does NOT beat a gradient-boosted / 3-rule baseline on free data** → direct, same-domain
     corroboration of CR-02 / the hardened TWO-BASELINE rule (fixed rule + persistence/GB). Every
     TRINETRA model claim MUST be stated as "beats GB + decision-tree baseline under tile-withheld
     spatial-CV" or it is an overclaim (Rule 16). This is the strongest CR-02 evidence in the corpus
     because it is OUR hazards, OUR region, OUR free-data constraint.
  3. **Honest achievable skill is MODEST** (ROC 0.73–0.89 best cases; some tasks at chance) → calibrates
     our lead-time / accuracy expectations to reality (Rule 19). We should present TRINETRA value as
     calibrated-probability + cost-loss decision support, NOT as a high-AUC oracle.
- **Methodology to adopt wholesale:** case-control matching against unfailed sites; block/tile-keyed
  folds; fixed seeds for digit-level reproducibility; attribution-by-deletion; physics monotonicity
  penalty (∂hazard/∂slope ≥ 0) — a concrete, cheap physics-gate implementation for our PINN layer
  (ties 2610.04828 PINN-FoS, Thread I).
- **Confirms our WHERE/WHEN split and the "two questions, never blended" discipline** — we should keep
  susceptibility (prior) and triggering (timing) as separate heads feeding the alarm policy, never a
  single blended score (also supports FL-MHSM 2604.16265's spatial prior being distinct from timing).
- **R6 (deformation ≠ susceptibility ranking)** is a crucial design correction: our InSAR layer should
  feed the WHEN/anomaly path and dam-instability flag, NOT be trusted to rank WHICH lake among peers —
  pair with WaveDINO/Rouet-Leduc InSAR notes.
- The **ranked watchlist "aid, not prediction"** framing is the exact human-in-the-loop, decision-support
  posture Rule 19 demands — a template for how TRINETRA should present outputs to authorities.

## Caveats / honesty
- Prediction TEST / feasibility, not an operational system — authors are explicit it is a prioritisation
  aid. Their modest ROCs are the CEILING on free data; a well-instrumented lake could do better (our
  ₹2000 ground layer is partly motivated by this ceiling).
- Nepal-centric; the 0.89 within-region burst result does NOT transfer across regions — reinforces our
  per-zone modelling and the need for Uttarakhand/Himachal-specific inventories.
- Small-flood susceptibility at chance (0.54) is a sober limit — some hazards may be unpredictable from
  free data alone; be honest about which TRINETRA layers can/cannot help each hazard.
