## What it is
A six-hour ENSEMBLE radar nowcasting model for EXTREME precipitation, built on the insight that
**storm-scale STRUCTURE is predictable far longer than individual cells**, so it:
- **jointly learns a DETERMINISTIC predictor** for the organised precipitation structure shared across
  ensemble members, PLUS
- **a GENERATOR (flow-matching) that produces diverse local RESIDUALS** — the uncertain local growth,
  decay, reorganisation and storm initiation — around the shared deterministic prediction.
- **Results (as published):** across INDEPENDENT test data from the US, Europe and China, MW-Nowcast
  beats leading methods (PySTEPS, SimVPv2, NowcastNet) for heavy+extreme precip throughout the 6 h
  horizon; **for the most intense rainfall it DOUBLES available warning time (6 h skill where the
  leading generative baseline was limited to 3 h), across all three regions.**
- **Evaluation includes:** grid metrics, probabilistic metrics (CRPS etc.), a **RELATIVE ECONOMIC VALUE
  / cost-loss decision analysis**, and a structural diagnostic. Cost-loss analysis shows MW-Nowcast
  "retains substantial value … even at 4–6 h, where alternative methods offer little benefit."

## Why it matters to TRINETRA (cost-loss evaluation + residual decomposition + lead-time honesty, all at once)
- **Independent, state-of-the-art endorsement of the EXACT evaluation philosophy we adopted.** They
  score models by **relative economic value under a cost-loss model** — the same C_FA/C_miss decision
  framing behind our Bayes p*=0.0476 and our "beat two baselines under cost-loss + calibrated
  uncertainty" PROCESS target (Rule 16). This is a top-tier precedent that **cost-loss / economic value
  is the right headline metric for a warning system**, not CSI/AUC alone. Cite as methodological anchor
  for our evaluation protocol.
- **Reinforces the deterministic-structure + generative-RESIDUAL decomposition** (Thread I "physics as
  structure, learner for residual"; also NowcastNet, TUPANN, CasCast). The split — predict the
  predictable shared structure deterministically, model ONLY the uncertain part stochastically — is a
  clean, reusable template for our rainfall front-end AND, by analogy, for the anomaly layer (predict
  normal structure, treat residual as the uncertain/anomalous part). Flow-matching for the residual is
  a concrete generative-ensemble option.
- **Honest lead-time ceiling, quantified.** Even this large model only reaches 6 h for extreme rain,
  and only by halving the gap from a 3 h baseline — i.e. **convective extreme-precip predictability is
  intrinsically short.** This is a crucial reality check for TRINETRA's cloudburst/flash-flood lead-time
  claims (Rule 16/19): hours, not days; and the value is in the ENSEMBLE spread + cost-loss decision,
  not a deterministic point forecast. Pairs with Thread J (onset timing hard).
- **Ensemble + CRPS = our UQ-first spine (T5).** They quantify reliability with calibrated probabilistic
  metrics; we should likewise report CRPS/rank-histogram on any rainfall forecast feeding the alarm.
- Baselines PySTEPS (optical-flow) + NowcastNet give us a concrete, reproducible BASELINE SET for the
  rainfall module — directly usable for our two-baseline rule.

## Caveats / honesty
- **RADAR-based** (US/Europe/China dense radar) — TRINETRA's Himalaya has NO such radar; we rely on
  satellite (IMERG). So MW-Nowcast is a METHOD/EVALUATION template, NOT a deployable model for us; the
  satellite-only analogue is TUPANN (2511.05471). The 6 h / doubled-warning-time numbers are radar-regime
  and do NOT transfer to our IMERG inputs — do not quote them as achievable for TRINETRA.
- 6 h is for extreme precip generally; a localized Himalayan CLOUDBURST may be even less predictable.
- Industrial model (Microsoft), large compute — architecture ideas transfer, scale does not (Kaggle
  budget). Take the det+residual PATTERN and the cost-loss EVALUATION, not the model size.
