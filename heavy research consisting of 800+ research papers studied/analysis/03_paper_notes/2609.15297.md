## What it is
Formalizes and defends against **adversarial feature missingness** in RL: an adversary **occludes a
limited number of state features** (the MOST important ones) from the agent's observation to minimize
return — the worst-case version of sensor/modality dropout.
- Positions adversarial missingness **between** two known concepts: ℓ∞-norm-bounded adversarial
  perturbations (features corrupted) and learning-with-missing-data (features randomly absent). Here
  features are absent AND chosen adversarially.
- **Admissable** = an adversarial-training algorithm (train against a worst-case masking adversary).
- Evaluated on 3 MuJoCo environments; **beats a baseline trained with random-uniform missingness** on
  all three (i.e., training against WORST-CASE missingness generalizes better than training against
  RANDOM missingness). Motivated explicitly by EU Trustworthy-AI deployment guidelines.
- Builds on robust-RL-on-state-observations line (Zhang et al. 2003.08938 / 2101.08452 — logged as
  seminal robust-RL anchors).

## Why it matters to TRINETRA (missing-modality CR + WS5 robustness)
- **This is the missing-modality failure mode made adversarial.** In a real Himalayan disaster the
  most informative sensors are exactly the ones likely to drop FIRST: the landslide destroys the tilt/
  LoRa node on the moving slope; cloud blocks Sentinel-2; the flood takes out the River-Eye camera;
  cellular/mesh backhaul fails. That is not random missingness — the hazard preferentially removes the
  highest-signal features right when they matter → **worst-case (adversarial) missingness is the
  correct threat model for TRINETRA**, not random dropout.
- **Design mandate for WS5:** train/evaluate the alarm policy under WORST-CASE feature masking (drop
  the k most-informative modalities), not just random imputation. The paper's key empirical lesson —
  **random-missingness training under-prepares you vs worst-case training** — becomes our rule: a
  model robust to random sensor loss can still fail when the hazard knocks out the decisive sensor.
- **Graceful degradation requirement (ties PIER 2603.17319):** the policy must degrade safely on the
  REMAINING local observations. Combine: (a) adversarial-missingness training (Admissable), (b)
  forecast-independent/local-obs policy (PIER), (c) hard physics shield as the floor when too many
  modalities are gone. → strengthens the WS5 composition and gives a concrete new CR / eval axis.
- Feeds a new contradiction/registry entry: **"random vs worst-case missingness"** — our imputation/
  dropout handling must be tested adversarially.

## Caveats / honesty
- MuJoCo continuous-control benchmarks, not a geohazard POMDP; the THREAT MODEL (adversarial vs random
  missingness) + the training lesson transfer; MuJoCo numbers do not.
- Small env set (3 tasks); demonstrates the principle, not a large-scale guarantee.
