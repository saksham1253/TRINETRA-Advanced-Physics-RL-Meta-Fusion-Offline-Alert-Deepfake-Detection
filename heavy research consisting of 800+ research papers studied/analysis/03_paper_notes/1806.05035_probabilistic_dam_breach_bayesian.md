## Content (own words)
- Problem/scope: dam-breach models predict the OUTFLOW HYDROGRAPH of a failing dam → key input
  to downstream flood-risk + evacuation planning. Focus: homogeneous, non-cohesive EARTHFILL
  embankment dams; process modeled = breach ENLARGEMENT driven by progressive surface erosion
  (NOT breach initiation or downstream propagation).
- Method: a SIMPLIFIED physics-based model with FEW parameters; erosion rate via empirical
  sediment-transport formulas (Meyer-Peter-Müller; Strickler friction). Embed in a BAYESIAN
  MULTILEVEL framework; invert against literature data of observed peak discharge + final breach
  width from HISTORICAL dam failures using MARKOV-CHAIN MONTE CARLO (MCMC); non-informative
  priors. Quantifies multiple uncertainty categories.
- Findings: dominant uncertainty = a CORRELATED subset of params (residual error term +
  epistemic breach-erosion-rate term). Posterior prediction intervals for peak discharge + final
  breach width are CONGRUENT with literature. A fully-probabilistic hydrograph prediction →
  better downstream risk management.

## Relevance to us
- Component: GLOF / dam-break / moraine-dam failure layer (TRINETRA's glacial-lake outburst
  pathway — e.g. South Lhonak 2023). A moraine-dam GLOF is physically a breach-enlargement
  erosion process → this model class is DIRECTLY applicable to estimating the GLOF outflow
  hydrograph that drives downstream people-at-risk + lead time. Complements the runout papers
  (2401.13695 GNS inverse runout, 2404.15860 FEM-MPM tailings, 2507.15021 ALPINE PINN flood).
- KEY design value: the BAYESIAN/MCMC UNCERTAINTY QUANTIFICATION is exactly what a life-safety
  EWS needs — not a single hydrograph but PREDICTION INTERVALS → feeds calibrated people-at-risk

  parsimony. The finding that uncertainty concentrates in erosion-rate + residual terms tells us
  WHERE to put calibration effort for GLOF forecasting.
- Honesty caveat: homogeneous earthfill dams ≠ natural moraine dams (ice cores, heterogeneous
