## What it is
First **open-source 5G** emergency-alert (Public Warning System, PWS) **spoofing attack**: modifies
OpenAirInterface (OAI) RAN + software-defined radio + a custom network-management system to forge
warning SIB messages. Analyzes how different smartphones behave. Then proposes a **lightweight
cross-cell verification** defense in OAI: the device compares the received warning against
neighboring cells' broadcasts and flags a **single-source alert as suspicious**.

## Key technical facts (as-published)
- PWS (3GPP) delivers alerts via **SIB messages, broadcast WITHOUT authentication or acknowledgment**
  — by design, to maximize reach without requiring a subscription. This is the structural
  vulnerability: any fake base station can impersonate the network and broadcast forged alerts.
- Country-specific instances: CMAS (US), ETWS (Japan earthquake/tsunami). India's analog is the NDMA
  Cell Broadcast / SACHET system (our last-mile channel).
- Impacts of spoofing: public panic, misinformation, phishing links — amplified because users under
  stress verify less.
- Defense = **cross-cell verification**: a genuine alert appears on multiple neighboring cells; a
  spoofed one from a single fake base station does not → flag single-source.

## Why it matters to TRINETRA (WS3 last-mile alerting + Rule 19)
- **Security of the alert channel is a first-class design concern, not an afterthought.** If
  TRINETRA routes alerts via CAP → SACHET/Cell-Broadcast, the channel itself is spoofable. A forged
  "evacuate now" or a forged "all clear" is a life-safety threat (Rule 19).
- **Design actions for WS3:**
  1. Treat inbound/outbound alert authenticity as a threat model item; prefer **signed CAP messages**
     (CAP supports XML digital signatures) end-to-end.
  2. For the cell-broadcast leg (unauthenticated by 3GPP design), adopt the paper's **cross-cell /
     multi-source corroboration** idea: a trustworthy client should see the same alert from multiple
     independent channels (CB + app + SMS) before treating it as authoritative.
  3. Our offline-first mesh (LoRa/ESP-NOW) legs need their own message authentication (HMAC/signed
     payloads) — add to WS7 secure-embedded-messaging requirements.
- Reinforces that "complement, don't replace, certified systems" (Rule 19) must include **not
  degrading trust** in the official channel.
